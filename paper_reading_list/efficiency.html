Distill video diffusion into a flow map between any two times, refined on-policy by flow map backward simulation: one model, any budget.
Insight. A distilled sampler should stay on the teacher's ODE: endpoint consistency re-noises its way off it and gets worse with more steps, while a two-time flow map keeps the ODE's test-time scaling, and its composition rule makes any-budget on-policy rollouts just three model calls.

  • Flow map (§3.1, Eq. 2). The PF-ODE dzt/dt=v Eq. (1) has an exact transport Φr←t(zt)=zr, composing as Φq←r∘Φr←t=Φq←t; a network learns fθ(zt,t,r)≈zr Eq. (2) for 1≥t>r≥0, with fθ(zt,t,t)=zt. CMs are the r=0 slice, flow matching the t=r one.

  • Why not consistency (§4.1, Fig. 1). A consistency sampler re-noises after each endpoint jump, so added steps drift off the PF-ODE instead of refining it: rCM falls 82.81 → 75.72 from 2 to 32 NFEs while the teacher climbs 62.65 → 83.41; a flow map keeps the ODE's scaling.

  • MeanFlow target without a JVP (§3.1, Eq. 3). fθ=zt−(t−r)uθ(zt,r,t), trained by ‖uθ−sg(utgt)‖22, utgt=v−(t−r)duθ/dt Eq. (3); the derivative a central difference (u(zt+ϵ,t+ϵ)−u(zt−ϵ,t−ϵ))/2ϵ Eq. (4), two extra forward passes, ϵ=5 of 1,000 in the release.

  • Interpolated timestep conditioning (§4.2.1). The second time enters as g·emb(t)+(1−g)·emb′(r), g=0.25, emb′ a copy of the pretrained emb: at t=r the model is the teacher. TMD's zero-initialized projection inflates the embedding norm and oversaturates frames (Fig. 11).

  • Time sampler and weight (§4.2.1, Fig. 10). t,r~U(0,1) sorted so t>r, Wan's timestep shift 5; half the batch at t=r, a quarter at r=0 (release); the weight w(t)=Beta(2,1.5) beats uniform at 32 NFEs, 83.96 vs 83.75; uniform mismatches the pretraining logit-normal.

  • Guidance fused into the prediction (Eq. 6). u=(uc−(1−g)sg(u∅))/g Eq. (6) with the teacher's scale g=3, so CFG is dropped at test time; MeanFlow fused it into the target instead. The finite difference is divided by g too, so prediction and derivative share the one scale.

  • Adaptive reweighting (Eq. 7). wt,r=1 at t=r, else μt=r/(‖Δ‖22+c) Eq. (7) with μt=r the mean boundary loss: transitions are scaled to the already-trained instantaneous field. Stage 1: 256K Wan2.1-14B synthetic clips, LoRA rank 256, 6k iterations (1.3B) or 4k (14B).

  • Flow map backward simulation (§4.2.2, Eq. 8). Composition fθ(zt,t,q)≈fθ(fθ(zt,t,r),r,q) Eq. (8) makes a rollout three calls: shortcut T→t, target step t→r, shortcut r→0, all with gradient; N∈{2,4,8,16,50}, any budget at one cost (Tab. 5: −47% at 16 steps, +22% at 4).

  • DMD on the rollout (§3.2, Eq. 5). Re-noise z^0 at t~U and push it by ∇θℒ=−𝔼[(sreal−sfake)∂fθ/∂θ] Eq. (5): the teacher at CFG 3 is the real score, a LoRA copy refit on student samples the fake, both read at r=t; the forward loss stays on alongside (lr 2×10−6, 800 iterations).

  • Causal variant (§4.3). On FAR: three full chunks at kernel 2, the rest at 4; a one-frame first chunk then chunks of three serve T2V and I2V. The instantaneous field survives, so the student keeps training on new domains (robotics, driving) where Self-Forcing cannot (Fig. 7–8).

  • Experiments (§5, Tab. 2–4). VBench on Wan2.1, 480×832, 81 frames, rCM, Self-Forcing and community causal models re-run under one protocol: 1.3B bidirectional 83.48 at 4 NFEs (rCM 82.51) and 83.96 at 32; 14B 84.04 at 4 (rCM 83.73); causal FAR-14B 84.05 at 4 → 84.41 at 32 (Krea-Realtime 83.25); I2V 87.87 at 4 NFEs against Wan2.1-I2V-14B's 87.71 at 50×2, so each student beats its 50×2-step teacher.

  • The sampler matters more than the init (Tab. 2). Flow-map training under the consistency backward simulation still decays at 32 NFEs, 83.55 → 82.96, and consistency ODE-init under it collapses, 82.96 → 79.80; the flow-map simulation is the only row rising, 83.48 → 83.96.

  • Synthetic data leaves a mark (§6). Every stage trains on Wan2.1-14B samples, never the base model's data, and the paper concedes smoother textures as the visible price; the semantic score trails the quality score everywhere, 76.41 against 85.24 at 1.3B and 4 NFEs.

  t, r = sort(rand(bs), rand(bs), descending=True)  # Eq. (2): r = t for 50% of the batch, r = 0 for 25%

  e = randn_like(x)
  z = (1 - t) * x + t * e
  v = e - x

  # 2. Predict the average velocity with guidance fused into the prediction
  u_c = model(z, c, t, r)
  with no_grad():
      u_n = model(z, null, t, r)
  u = (u_c - (1 - 3.0) * u_n) / 3.0  # Eq. (6); g = 3, the teacher's CFG scale

  # 3. du/dt by a central difference along v, no JVP
  eps = 5 / 1000  # 5 of 1,000 timesteps
  with no_grad():
      dudt = (model(z + eps * v, c, t + eps, r) - model(z - eps * v, c, t - eps, r)) / (2 * eps * 3.0)  # Eq. (4)
  u_tgt = v - (t - r) * dudt  # Eq. (3)

  # 4. Weighted loss, transitions rescaled to the boundary loss
  loss = ((u - u_tgt.detach()) ** 2).flatten(1).mean(1) * beta_weight(t)  # w(t) = t (1 - t)^0.5, Beta(2, 1.5)
  mu = loss[r == t].mean()
  loss = where(r == t, loss, loss * mu / (loss.detach() + 1e-5))  # Eq. (7); c = 1e-5
  loss = loss.mean()


def distill():
  # 1. Pick a budget N and the step j that carries the gradient
  N = choice([2, 4, 8, 16, 50])  # paper: s ~ U{1..T}; the release draws from this list
  j = randint(0, N)
  ts = cat([shift(linspace(1, 0, N + 1)[:-1], 5), [0]])
  z = randn_like(x)

  # 2. Flow map backward simulation: shortcut, target step, shortcut, gradient through all three
  for a, b in ((ts[0], ts[j]), (ts[j], ts[j + 1]), (ts[j + 1], ts[-1])):  # Eq. (8)
      if a != b:
          z = z - (a - b) * model(z, c, a, b)

  # 3. DMD gradient on the re-noised rollout; the fake model is a LoRA copy of the teacher refit on z
  with no_grad():
      t = shift(rand(bs), 5)
      z_t = (1 - t) * z + t * randn_like(z)
      x_fake = z_t - t * fake_model(z_t, c, t, t)
      x_c, x_n = z_t - t * teacher(z_t, c, t, t), z_t - t * teacher(z_t, null, t, t)
      x_real = x_c + 3.0 * (x_c - x_n)  # CFG as the release writes it
      grad = (x_fake - x_real) / abs(z - x_real).mean()  # Eq. (5), normalized as in DMD2
  loss = mse(z, (z - grad).detach()) + train()  # forward loss co-trained; lr 2e-6 for student and fake model

A main backbone once per transition, a recurrent flow head for cheap inner steps — a MeanFlow map pretrained, then distilled with DMD2.
Insight. A few-step student need not be monolithic: spend the big network on semantics once per transition and iterate only the last blocks, so NFEs turn fractional — and let the distribution-matching gradient flow through the unrolled inner steps, or the extra steps buy nothing on video.

  • Transition matching (§2, Eq. 1–6). On xt=(1−t)x+tx1 Eq. (1) flow matching predicts v=𝔼[x1−x∣x] Eq. (2) in many small steps; TM models the jump xti→xti−1 through a latent y:=x1−x Eq. (3), xti−1=xti−(ti−ti−1)y Eq. (4), with y drawn by an inner flow ys=(1−s)y+sy1 Eq. (5) of velocity 𝔼[y1−(x1−x)∣y,xti] Eq. (6) from a light head on backbone features; DTM still needs ~30 steps.

  • The two tools (§2, Eq. 7–11). A flow map f(ys,s,r)=ys−∫srvdτ Eq. (7) through an average velocity obeys u+(s−r)du/ds=v Eq. (8), trained by ‖uθ−u^‖2, u^=sg(v−(s−r)duθ/ds) Eq. (9–10); DMD2 matches distributions, 𝔼[w(t)sg(D(x^t,t))⊤x^] Eq. (11) with D the fake-minus-teacher score — flow maps alone struggle on video, whose Wan paths curve near t=1, the high-noise end where a map must jump farthest.

  • Decoupled student (§3, Eq. 12, Tab. 10). The teacher's first L−H blocks become the main backbone mθ(xti,ti), its last H=5 the flow head ysj−1≈fθ(ysj,sj,sj−1;mθ(xti,ti)) Eq. (12) on an inner grid 0=s0<…<sN=1; the target y=x1−x beats y=x, 84.68 to 84.18.

  • Gated fusion (App. A.1, Fig. 8, Tab. 9). ys passes through the main patch embedding and an AdaLN block on s, then blends into mt through a learned sigmoid gate, so the student's first forward equals the teacher's; concatenation ties it, 84.76 to 84.68, but trains unstably.

  • Stage 1, TM-MF (§3.1, Eq. 13–14). fθ(ys,s,r;m):=ys+(s−r)uθ(ys,s,r;m) Eq. (13), uθ:=y1−headθ(ys,s,r;m) Eq. (14), so at r→s the head still outputs the teacher's velocity; the MeanFlow loss takes the conditional y1−y, the backbone is not detached, and the JVP is a central difference, δ=0.005, as FSDP and flash attention forbid forward-mode AD, with ys±δ=ys±δv and one-sided differences at the ends.

  • Inner clocks and knobs (App. A.2). The head grid sj has a shift of 10; training draws s uniformly, shifts it by 3, and sets r to the grid point below — discrete pairs mirroring inference, stabilizing it; 75% of batches at r=s, CFG 3, 3k steps. Plain TM pretraining ends 0.07–0.38 lower.

  • Stage 2, DMD2-v (§3.2, Tab. 3–4). Two of three: a Conv3D discriminator head on teacher features (83.24, against 82.32 Conv1D–2D and 81.63 without GAN), and KD warm-up for one-step students only — at two steps it plants coarse artifacts DMD never removes, 83.79 vs 84.39.

  • Timestep shifting (§3.2, Tab. 5, Fig. 9). The third: t=γt′/((γ−1)t′+1) on both clocks, γ=5 on the DMD noise level and 10 on the student's steps (84.39 against 83.44); without the first, every video puts its subject on the left — a mode collapse VBench scores at 83.22.

  • Flow head rollout (§3.2, Eq. 15). Unroll the inner flow into a generator, gθ(xti,ti;y1):=x1−InnerFlow(mθ(xti,ti)) Eq. (15), and pass the VSD gradient through all inner steps — backward simulation, undetached, under 17% more compute at N=2; without it training is slow.

  • Effective NFE and inference (§4.1, Eq. 16–18, Alg. 1). NFE:=M(1+(N−1)H/L) Eq. (16): M transitions of one full pass plus N−1 head passes. Sampling draws fresh y1 per transition, runs the head N times, steps x←x−(ti−ti−1)y^0; outer steps are deterministic Eq. (18).

  • Experiments (§4, Tab. 1–2). Wan2.1 1.3B and 14B text-to-video at 480×832, 81 frames, 500k teacher clips, VBench: N2H5 at 2.33 NFE 84.68 against rCM's 84.43 at 4 NFE and DMD2-v's 84.39; at 1.17 NFE 83.80 against rCM's 82.65; on 14B 84.24 at 1.38 NFE against 83.02.

  • Fractional steps pay at one outer step, not two, on 14B (Tab. 2). TMD-N4H5 at 2.75 NFE scores 84.62, under two-step rCM's 85.05 and DMD2-v's 84.79, while at M=1 the same head lifts one-step rCM's 83.02 to 84.24; on 1.3B the gain does survive at M=2 (Fig. 6).

A network-free policy integrates the ODE in dense substeps between the student's few calls, trained by on-policy imitation of the teacher.
Insight. Decouple network calls from integration steps: give the student a closed-form object whose rollout has the teacher's format, and distillation collapses to a squared velocity match along the student's own trajectory — no JVP, no auxiliary network, and the teacher's diversity survives.

  • Flow matching ties cost to precision (§2, Eq. 1–2). With xt=(1−t)x0+tϵ, the probability-flow ODE is x˙t=(xt−𝔼[x0∣xt])/t Eq. (1) and Gθ(xt,t) fits it by 𝔼12‖u−Gθ(xt,t)‖2, u=(xt−x0)/t Eq. (2) — every velocity query is a network call, so accuracy and NFEs rise together.

  • Policy-based flow (§3). A trajectory is fixed by its start (xtsrc,tsrc), so a network-free π can describe it; the student π←Gϕ(xtsrc,tsrc) emits it once and xtdst=xtsrc+∫πdt runs in 128 substeps — few network calls, dense integration, and a rollout in the teacher's own format.

  • DX policy (§3.1, Tab. 1). π=(xt−x^0(t))/t, the posterior mean x^0 read off a grid of N predictions at even times in [tdst,tsrc] and linearly interpolated: any N-step teacher path fits, but x^0 cannot react to a perturbed xt, and FID swings 4.44–4.90 with N between 10 and 40.

  • GMFlow policy (§3.2, Thm. 1). The network predicts a Gaussian mixture over velocity, q(u∣xtsrc)=∏i∑k=1KAik𝒩(ui;μik,s2I), one C-channel mixture per pixel; K=N·C components fit any N-step trajectory, K=8 suffices, and the posterior adapts to xt: 4.44 becomes 3.07.

  • The closed-form velocity (App. F, Eq. 21–28). In x0 units, μxk=xtsrc−σtsrcμk, sx=σtsrcs Eq. (21), the posterior at a later (xt,t) is again a mixture Eq. (23) of variance s′2=sx2/(sx2ζ+1) Eq. (24): with ν=αtxt/σt2−αtsrcxtsrc/σtsrc2, ζ=αt2/σt2−αtsrc2/σtsrc2, means μk′=(sx2ν+μxk)/(sx2ζ+1) Eq. (25), logits ak′=logAk+μxk·(ν−12ζμxk)/(sx2ζ+1) Eq. (27), and π(xt,t)=(xt−∑kAk′μk′)/t Eq. (28).

  • π-ID, on-policy imitation (§4, Alg. 1). πD=stopgrad(π); draw t~U(tdst,tsrc), roll the detached policy to xt=xtsrc+∫tsrctπDdt, add 12‖Gθ(xt,t,c)−π(xt,t)‖2 — DAgger-style: the teacher corrects states the student itself visits, error O(nϵ); two states per forward.

  • Data-dependent or data-free starts (Alg. 2–3, App. B.4). xtsrc is real data noised at a grid time τsrc∈{1/NFE,…,1}, shifted by t=mτ/(1+(m−1)τ), m=3.2 at 1 MP, or the detached policy's rollout from noise across all segments; same cost and FID (29.8 vs 29.7).

  • GM dropout and temperature (§5.1, App. B.1). Training drops components of πD at p=0.05 (0.1 at 1 MP), so rollouts explore new modes: 4.14 → 3.65 FID. Inference sharpens it, Ak1/T, variance s2T Eq. (4), T=0.3 at 4 NFE, 0.7 at 8, off on the last step; ablated, 14.3 → 14.9.

  • Micro-window matching (App. B.3, Eq. 6). The target becomes the policy's mean velocity over a window, 12‖Gθ(xt,t)−1Δt∫t−Δttπdt‖2, Δt=3/128 Eq. (6): smoother signal, a stable DX policy, and a 43-step teacher — sharper than FLUX.1 dev at 128; ablated, 14.3 → 14.6.

  • Scheduled trajectory mixing (App. B.2). FLUX.1 dev, guidance-distilled without real CFG, cannot steer stray states back; training starts off-policy on teacher rollouts, matching segment mean velocities Eq. (5), and mixes in policy segments as the teacher ratio decays to 0 in 2k.

  • Experiments (§5, Tab. 2–4). ImageNet-256 FID-50K, SiT-XL/2 teachers: 1 NFE 2.85 (REPA), 3.34 (FM) against MeanFlow's 3.43, 2 NFE 1.97; FLUX.1-12B and Qwen-Image-20B at 4 NFE: teacher-referenced FID 14.3 against SenseFlow's 23.3, OneIG diversity 0.229 vs 0.151.

  • Distribution-matching students repeat themselves (Fig. 4, Tab. 4). SenseFlow and Qwen-Image Lightning win on preference (HPSv2 0.322 vs 0.314) yet their OneIG diversity falls to 0.151 and 0.116 against the teachers' 0.238 and 0.194; pi-Flow holds 0.229 and 0.180.

  • Fewer teacher steps make sharper images (Fig. 9). FLUX.1 dev at 128 NFE renders blurrier than at 43, and a student inherits whatever its matching window implies — the window is a quality knob as much as a stabilizer, and the teacher's best sampler is not its densest.

def gm_velocity(x_t, t, mu_x, var_x, logweights, x_src, t_src):
  # The policy: the posterior over x_0 at a later state, closed form from the mixture predicted at the start
  nu = (1 - t) * x_t / t ** 2 - (1 - t_src) * x_src / t_src ** 2  # below Eq. (22)
  zeta = (1 - t) ** 2 / t ** 2 - (1 - t_src) ** 2 / t_src ** 2
  mu = (var_x * nu + mu_x) / (var_x * zeta + 1)  # Eq. (25)
  logits = logweights + (mu_x * (nu - 0.5 * zeta * mu_x)).sum(channels) / (var_x * zeta + 1)  # Eq. (27)
  x_0 = (softmax(logits, k) * mu).sum(k)
  return (x_t - x_0) / t  # Eq. (28)


def train():
  # 1. Pick the segment: a grid time tau_src in {1/NFE, ..., 1} with the last step halved, then the 1 MP shift
  tau_src, tau_dst = sample_segment(bs, nfe=4, final_step_scale=0.5)  # Alg. 2 lines 4-5
  t_src = 3.2 * tau_src / (1 + 2.2 * tau_src)  # line 6; shift m = 3.2
  x_src = (1 - t_src) * x_0 + t_src * randn_like(x_0)  # line 7; data-free rolls the policy from noise instead

  # 2. One student forward emits the policy: K = 8 Gaussians per pixel over the velocity, converted to x_0 units
  means, logstds, logweights = model(x_src, t_src, c)  # line 8; LoRA rank 256 on the FFNs plus a wider output layer

  policy = GMPolicy(mu_x, var_x, logweights, x_src, t_src)  # pi(x_t, t) = gm_velocity(...)
  rollout = policy.detach().dropout(0.1)  # line 9; GM dropout masks components to -inf and renormalizes

  # 3. Two intermediate states on the detached rollout; the teacher's velocity there is the target
  loss = 0
  for tau in sort(tau_dst + 3 / 128 + rand(2) * (tau_src - tau_dst - 3 / 128), descending=True):  # line 11
      x_t, t = rollout.integrate(x_src, t_src, to=tau, substep=1 / 128)  # line 13; Euler on gm_velocity

  • Frozen real score (Eq. 4). The teacher, frozen and read as a score: sreal(xt,t)=−(xt−αtμbase(xt,t))/σt2 — the prediction minus the input, scaled, because a trained denoiser is a score estimator. It never moves, so preal is whatever the teacher learned, whose ceiling is DMD's.

  • Learned fake score (Eq. 5–6). The same formula over a second copy μfakeϕ, retrained continually on the generator's own output by plain denoising, tracking a distribution that moves as the generator learns. GAN-like, two nets chasing — but no discriminator, no minimax.

  • Gradient weighting (Eq. 8). Scales that gradient so its magnitude is comparable across noise levels, using the teacher's own error on the sample. It reads like a footnote and is worth ~1 FID: DreamFusion's and ProlificDreamer's weightings give 3.60 / 3.71 on CIFAR-10, against 2.66.

  • Regression loss (Eq. 9). LPIPS against noise–image pairs sampled from the teacher's ODE solver, at under 1% of the compute. Needed because a score ignores rescaling of p: mode dropping is a fixed point, not an instability. Ablated: 2.62 → 5.61; DMD2 drops it.

  • Alternating update (Alg. 1). Gθ on DKL+0.25ℒreg, unpaired samples for the first and paired for the second; μfakeϕ on denoising, one update each per step. Three networks resident at once — the memory limit the paper names, and which it suggests LoRA could one day relieve.

  • Classifier-free guidance (§3.4). Pairs and sreal come from the guided teacher, sfake is left alone; the scale is baked in: no CFG knob.

  • Experiments (§4). ImageNet-64 and CIFAR-10 by FID, MS COCO-30K by FID and CLIP after distilling SD v1.5 on LAION-Aesthetics: one step reads 2.62 on ImageNet, within 0.3 of the 512-step EDM teacher, and 11.49 on COCO against SD's 8.78 — 0.09s an image, 30× faster.

It halves a sampler's steps by distillation, over and over — 8192 down to 4 — introducing velocity prediction to keep few-step models stable.
Insight. Halve the steps and re-teach at logarithmic cost, pick a parameterization that survives low SNR: velocity prediction outgrew distillation.

  • One student step = two teacher steps (§3, Alg. 2). The student starts as a copy of the teacher and trains like a diffusion model except for the target: from zt, run two teacher DDIM steps, then invert one student DDIM step to get the x~ that reaches the same point in one jump.

  • Why the target beats real data (§3). Given zt the real x is ambiguous — many images produce the same noisy input, so standard training regresses to a blurry average. The target x~ is fully determined by teacher and zt: sharp, which lets one step do two steps' work.

  • The halving ladder (§3). When the student converges it becomes the next teacher and N halves: CIFAR-10 descends 8192 to 4 at 50K updates per rung, no more than the original training cost. FID holds near-optimal to 4–8 steps — 3.00 at 4 — where DDIM needs 50 for 4.67.

  • Where noise prediction breaks (§4). Distillation pushes evaluation toward zero SNR, where x^=(zt−σtϵ^)/αt divides by a vanishing αt; at one step the input is pure noise, so ϵ^ is the input and says nothing about x — and the implicit SNR loss weighting gives that regime zero weight.

  • Velocity prediction (§4, App. D). Of three stable fixes — predict x; predict x and ϵ jointly; predict v≡αtϵ−σtx — the last is stablest: on the angle ϕ, a DDIM update becomes a rotation whose step size ignores SNR. New weightings ride along: truncated SNR, SNR+1.

  • Against one-shot distillation (§6). Luhman & Luhman regress a one-step student on precomputed full-length teacher runs — a cost linear in the teacher's steps. PD never runs the teacher at full length — cost logarithmic — and hits 9.12 at one step against their 9.36.

  • Experiments (§5.2). CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64, LSUN Bedroom and Church, from 8192 or 1024 steps, FID after every halving against DDIM and a stochastic sampler: near-optimal to 4–8 steps on all four, sharp loss only at 1–2; CIFAR-10 2.57 / 3.00 / 4.51 / 9.12 at 8 / 4 / 2 / 1 steps.

  • Parameterization matters less than stability (§5.1, Tab. 1). From scratch on CIFAR-10, every stable choice lands within ~0.1 FID of the rest, and x-prediction edges v, 2.51 against 2.75 with DDIM; v is chosen for the SNR-free step size, and ϵ with truncated SNR diverges.
