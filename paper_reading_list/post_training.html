A pair of trajectories from one noise, every step bent toward the reward-weighted image difference — the whole sampling run as one action.

  • Why per-step updates are mostly noise (§1, §3.2). In an MDP each stochastic step is an action reinforced by the trajectory's advantage: a small part of every update helps the reward, the rest drifts what no reward watches — styles cycle, grid artifacts appear past ~500 epochs.

  • The whole run is one action (§4). Rollouts x0:T, x^0:T from one noise ϵ with mild stochasticity; Δx=x^T−xT, ΔR=R(x^T)−R(xT), and the velocities at all steps of both bend toward ΔRΔx, which points from the worse image to the better — no step credited for its own noise.

  • Why an endpoint direction works mid-trajectory (§4.1, App. C.4). The update needs ∇R(xT)⊤Ji[ΔRΔx]≥0, Ji the Jacobian of the flow from step i; it holds in expectation if Ji is positive semi-definite — exact for optimal transport, which diffusion flows approximate, SDEdit's bet too.

  • A stochastic sampler that stays on schedule (Alg. 1, B.1). Overshoot the Euler step to lower noise, then add fresh noise back to ti+1 — EDM's trick, so the network's time stays right; γi, the noise fraction re-drawn, is 0.0025 everywhere. Flow-GRPO alone: 71.06 → 76.74 (Fig. 8 B):

t~i+1=ti+11−γiti+1+γi,x~i+1=xi+(t~i+1−ti)vi,xi+1=x~i+1+t~i+1γi2+2γiϵiγit~i+1+1

  • Normalize the difference (§4.3). ΔR is, to first order, proportional to ‖Δx‖, so ΔRΔx counts the pair's distance twice; the signal used is Δx―=Δx/(‖Δx‖RMS2+10−6), the difference over its own mean square. Without it 82.13 → 79.74 and training turns unstable (Fig. 8, H).

  • Clipping without a likelihood (Alg. 2). With no likelihood for PPO's ratio, each stored velocity gets the target vtarget=vref−Δx― and a proxy ratio ρ from two regressions; SPO then penalizes straying far from the rollout's velocity, ε=0.03. PPO ties (G, 81.99) only under the interval schedule:

ρ=exp(‖vtarget−vref‖2−‖vtarget−vcur‖2),L=−ΔRρ−|ΔR|2ε(ρ−1)2

  • Batching, and what is switched off (§4.3, Tab. 1). 432 prompt pairs per epoch at T=40, 34,560 stored samples, 4 batches, Flow-GRPO's memory; LoRA rank 32 on SD3.5-M; EMA off (strictly harmful), KL and CFG off, so the algorithms and not the regularizers are compared.

  • Uniform stochasticity wins (B.2, Fig. 20). Against constant γi (U), an interval schedule perturbs one random window (I), a prior schedule only the seed (P), with optional gradient weighting: U 82.13 / 84.25 at 200 epochs / best, I 82.29 / 83.50 yet weighting-sensitive, P 70.79 / 80.51.

  • Finite differences are a smoothed gradient (App. C). Perturb the step-j image, x′~𝒩(x,σ2I), finish both ODEs, form g~=(R(xT′)−R(xT))/σ2·(xT′−xT). With f the flow from j, J its Jacobian, Taylor and Stein's lemma make its expectation a smoothed gradient:

DDPO is denoising score matching with noisy targets; keep the pretraining loss instead and weight each sample by its advantage.

  • The two objectives do not match (§3.2). In LLMs both stages maximize one sequence log-likelihood; in diffusion, pretraining is flow matching on the forward process (Eq. 5) while DDPO, which Flow-GRPO inherits, maximizes a per-step reverse-transition Gaussian likelihood (Eq. 3–4).

  • DDPO is score matching in disguise (Thm 1, Eq. 6). The joint law of (xt−Δt,xt) is the same forward and reverse, so up to Euler–Maruyama error a DDPO step at t targets 𝔼‖sθ(xt,t)−∇logp(xt∣xt−Δt)‖2 — denoising score matching conditioned on a noisy intermediate instead.

  • The target is still the right one (Lemma 1). For any s<t, score matching conditioned on xs has the same population minimizer as the plain objective — the textbook clean-data result is the case s=0 — so what separates DDPO from pretraining is not bias but its noise.

  • Noisier conditioning, wider target (Thm 2). Both conditional scores are unbiased, yet conditioning on xs inflates the target covariance by κ(s,t)I, where κ(s,t)=(1−t)2s2/[t2(t2(1−s)2−s2(1−t)2)] is zero at s=0, climbs strictly with s, and diverges as s approaches t from below.

  • The cost shows without any RL (§3.3, Eq. 7–8). Replace EDM's clean target ‖Dθ−x0‖2 with the noisy proxy that mirrors DDPO and change nothing else: the run learns in the right direction but reaches every FID level later, so the penalty is the objective, not the RL machinery.

  • A different MDP (§4). DDPO's state is (c,t,xt), its action xt−1, its policy pθ(xt−1|xt); AWM's state is the prompt c, its action the finished image x0, its policy pθ(x0|c) — one decision per finished sample, not one per denoising step, which is what makes the trajectory disposable.

  • The matching loss is the log-likelihood (Eq. 10). logπθ(x0|c) is read through its ELBO surrogate −𝔼t[w(t)‖vθ(xt,t,c)−(ϵ−x0)‖2]; the uniform w(t)=1 beats the ELBO weight (1−t)/t of Eq. 2, visual quality and likelihood pulling apart here as they have ever since DDPM.

  • GRPO on that surrogate (Eq. 9, 11–12). The ratio becomes exp(−𝔼t[w(t)(‖vθ−v‖2−‖vθold−v‖2)]), with t and ϵ shared across the two policies to cut variance, and the KL a velocity-space ‖vθ−vθref‖2 — β=0.2 collapses into instability, 2.0 crawls, and 0.4 to 1.0 holds both ends.

  • What the gradient does, and what it frees. On-policy the update is −Ai∇θ𝔼t[w(t)‖vθ−(ϵ−x0)‖2]: a positive advantage pulls vθ toward that target, a negative one away. Sampling and training decouple — 20 sampler steps against 4 training ones on its grid, any solver, no CFG.

  • Experiments (§5). SD3.5-M and FLUX with LoRA on GenEval, OCR and PickScore, reward per GPU-hour vs Flow-GRPO: GenEval 0.95 matches it at 8.02× less compute, OCR 0.89 costs 17.6 GPU-hours against 415.9 (23.6×), PickScore 23.02 10.5× less; on FLUX 8.5× and 6.8×.

  • Where t is drawn from matters (§5.2, Fig. 7). Uniform and the sampler's grid tie; logit-normal, pretraining's default, lags, then degrades as RL runs on — mid-range mass is the wrong prior once the loss is advantage-weighted; reusing half the last policy's batches changes nothing.

Kaiwen Zheng (1,630), Huayu Chen (3,802), Haotian Ye (4,460), Haoxiang Wang (3,327), Qinsheng Zhang (5,126), Kai Jiang (281), Hang Su (24,066), Stefano Ermon (144,643), Jun Zhu (73,571), Ming-Yu Liu (55,621)

Online RL on the forward process: reward splits the rollouts into implicit positive and negative policies, and one flow-matching loss contrasts them.

  • Why the reverse process is a trap (§2.2). Diffusion admits no exact likelihood, so GRPO-style work discretizes the reverse SDE into tractable Gaussians — a policy gradient bought with first-order SDE samplers, stored trajectories and a model untied from its own forward process.

  • Split the rollouts by reward (§3.1). Read r∈[0,1] as the probability that a sample is good, p(o=1∣x0,c). The K images then weight two implicit policies, π+∝rπold and π−∝(1−r)πold: each image counts r toward the first and 1−r toward the second, and π+≻πold≻π−.

  • A direction, not a target (§3.2, Eq. 3). Improved velocity v*=vold+(1/β)Δ: the old velocity plus a correction Δ scaled by 1/β — CFG's shape, which the paper reads as the same move, done once and frozen: the conditional model as positive policy, unconditional as negative, w as 1/β.

  • Why the velocities align (Thm 3.1, Eq. 4). Bayes' rule gives the positive-group probability α=(πt+/πtold)𝔼πoldr. The posterior mixture makes vold=αv++(1−α)v−: the old velocity lies between the two at fixed noise level, position, and prompt. Thus Δ=α(v+−vold)=(1−α)(vold−v−) points toward the positive policy; substituting β=α>0 into v*=vold+Δ/β gives v*=v+, the reward-weighted positive policy.

  • One loss, two implicit policies (Eq. 5–6). ℒ=𝔼[r‖vθ+−v‖2+(1−r)‖vθ−−v‖2] over vθ±=(1∓β)vold±βvθ has optimum vold+(2/β)Δ (Thm 3.2): flow matching that has merely seen a reward, the guidance already in it. Delete the negative term and the reward collapses at once (§4.4).

  • Reward to probability (Alg. 1). r=1/2+1/2clip[(rraw−𝔼πoldrraw)/Zc,−1,1] with Zc a reward std — group-relative like GRPO, but landing on a split probability rather than an advantage, which is what lets a single batch weight the two branches by r and 1−r at every noise level.

  • Off-policy by construction (§3.3). The sampler is an EMA copy, θold←ηiθold+(1−ηi)θ, so no importance ratio is needed; on-policy η=0 collapses and η=0.9 crawls, while the ramp ηi=min(0.001i,0.5) holds both ends — only the OCR reward needs a 0.999 ceiling.

  • Weighting and guidance strength (§3.3, §4.4). The distillation method DMD's self-normalized x0 regression stands in for the weighting w(t): ‖xθ−x0‖2/sg(mean|xθ−x0|), beating every fixed schedule while w(t)=1−t collapses; β near 1 is stable, 0.1 buys a faster climb.

  • Solver freedom, and no CFG (§3.2, §4.4). Data collection is a black-box call, so the 2nd-order ODE sampler it permits beats the first-order SDE GRPO is pinned to, clearest on noise-sensitive PickScore; CFG is dropped outright and the policy starts from the conditional model alone.

  • Experiments (§4). CFG-free SD3.5-M (2.5B), five rewards in sequence, preferences first: at 1.7k iterations GenEval 0.94, OCR 0.91, PickScore 23.80, past guided SD3.5-L (8B) everywhere, FLUX.1-Dev (12B) on all but ClipScore; single rewards run 3–25× faster than FlowGRPO.

  • Positives alone collapse (§4.4). Without v− the reward collapses at once — unlike language models, where rejection-sampling fine-tuning on positives is a strong baseline — so the negative half is what diffusion RL cannot do without — the finding against the LLM playbook.

The released code fills in what the paper leaves out: the three policies are three rank-32 LoRA states of one frozen backbone, the training timesteps are the sampler's own 10-step grid rather than a prior, and an unmentioned anchor on the pretrained model rides along with the objective.

Concurrent with Flow-GRPO, it took GRPO to production scale across image and video; its shared-noise groups and timestep dropout are stock parts of video RL.

One GRPO recipe stretched across diffusion and rectified flow and across image, video and image-to-video, with the fixes each scale demanded.

  • Diffusion and rectified flow are one update (§2.1). Both read as z~s=z~t+(network output)(ηs−ηt); only the meaning of z~ and η changes — z/α and σ/α for ϵ-prediction, z and t for flow. One MDP then covers both, which is why a single recipe here can span all four base models.

  • Two SDEs, one purpose (Eq. 7–8). Diffusion gets dzt=[ftzt−1+εt22gt2∇logpt]dt+εtgtdw, rectified flow dzt=(ut−12εt2∇logpt)dt+εtdw; under a Gaussian pt the score is closed-form, so both give a Gaussian policy whose per-step likelihood is as cheap to read as DDPO's.

  • The objective, minus the KL (Eq. 9–10). Ai=(ri−mean{r})/std{r} applied at every timestep because the reward exists only at the last one, inside a clipped ratio. The KL is dropped outright — the paper reports no measurable difference from keeping it, so nothing anchors the policy.

  • One noise per group. All G samples for a prompt start from the same zT. Giving each its own noise, as DDPO does, reliably produces reward hacking and unstable training in video — the group comparison has to be about the policy, not about which seed happened to be lucky.

  • Timestep dropout. 40% of the denoising steps are masked at random each iteration. The first 30% carry the most signal, yet training only on them is worse than the full sequence, so a uniform random subset is the compromise that keeps late-stage refinement in the picture.

  • Several rewards, added as advantages. Reward models live on different scales, so it is the normalized advantages that are summed, Ai=∑k(rik−μk)/σk, not the rewards. HPS alone drives images toward an oily look; adding CLIP holds them back toward realism.

  • Best-of-N as a data filter (§3.2). Train on the best 8 and worst 8 of a pool of 16, 64 or 256 per prompt: the larger the pool the faster the reward climbs, paid for in sampling time — which is why the paper leaves it an optional extension rather than a part of the default recipe.

  • Binary rewards train (§3.2). Thresholding HPS at 0.28 and CLIP at 0.39 into {0,1} still moves the model, which is what makes rule-based rewards plausible for images at all, and echoes what verifiable rewards have already done for reasoning in the language models.

  • Experiments (§3). SD1.4, FLUX, HunyuanVideo and SkyReels-I2V on HPS-v2.1, CLIP and VideoAlign, 10K+ prompts: SD1.4 goes 0.239 → 0.365 on HPS, past DDPO's 0.297 and ReFL's 0.357; HunyuanVideo gains 56% visual and 181% motion quality, SkyReels-I2V 91% motion.

  • What it costs, said plainly (§3.2–3.3). Optimizing one reward moves the others: FLUX tuned on HPS alone falls 0.659 → 0.561 on GenEval, and HunyuanVideo's text alignment drops 1.75 → 1.59 while its quality rises — VideoAlign's alignment head was too unstable to train against.

It brought online RL to flow models by turning their ODE into an SDE — the conversion that gave a flow a policy at all, and the start of every flow RL method since.

imageflowrl

A flow model has no randomness to explore with — convert its ODE into an equivalent SDE, and GRPO then carries over almost unchanged.

  • The MDP is inherited (§3). st=(c,t,xt), at=xt−1, π(at|st)=pθ(xt−1|xt,c), reward only at t=0 — DDPO's mapping taken whole. What is new is making a flow model fit into it at all, since a flow has no per-step conditional distribution to write down at all, let alone to differentiate.

  • A flow model has no policy (§4). Rectified-flow sampling is the ODE dxt=vtdt: the step is a bijection, so p(xt−1|xt) would need a divergence estimate, and beyond the initial seed there is nothing to explore — given the seed, the trajectory is fixed, every rollout the same.

  • ODE to SDE, the target (App. A, Eq. 10–14). The sampler is the ODE dxt=vtdt; wanted is an SDE dxt=fdt+σtdw with the same marginals pt. Each moves its density by its own law, Fokker–Planck or the continuity equation, so equal marginals means equal right-hand sides:

−∇·[fpt]+12∇2[σt2pt]=∂tpt=−∇·[vtpt]

  • The diffusion term is a drift in disguise (Eq. 15–17). Since ∇p=p∇logp, ∇2[σt2pt]=σt2∇·(pt∇logpt): what noise does to pt is a flow of pt along its own score. Fold it into the drift and the equation is solved by f=vt+(σt2/2)∇logpt, an SDE that spreads and re-gathers equally:

dxt=[vt(xt)+σt22∇logpt(xt)]dt+σtdw(forward SDE, Eq. 17)

  • Run it backwards (Eq. 18–21). Generation runs t=1→0, noise to data, so the reverse-time SDE is what is sampled: Anderson's rule turns dx=fdt+gdw into dx=[f−g2∇logpt]dt+gdw¯, and with g=σt the score's coefficient flips, σt2/2−σt2=−σt2/2 — the main text's Eq. 7:

dxt=[vt(xt)−σt22∇logpt(xt)]dt+σtdw(backward SDE, Eq. 21)

  • First the score (Eq. 22–24). No network outputs ∇logpt, but for xt=αtx0+βtx1 the conditional pt(xt|x0)=𝒩(αtx0,βt2I) has score −(xt−αtx0)/βt2=−x1/βt, and the marginal score is its posterior average — ∇pt=∫p(x0)∇pt(xt|x0)dx0, divided through by pt:

∇logpt(xt)=𝔼[∇logpt(xt|x0)|xt]=−𝔼[x1|xt]βt

  • Then the velocity (Eq. 25–27). vt(x)=𝔼[α˙tx0+β˙tx1|xt=x]; write x0=(xt−βtx1)/αt and every term is x or 𝔼[x1|xt], which Eq. 24 turns into the score. With αt=1−t, βt=t the bracket collapses to t+t2/(1−t)=t/(1−t), and solving for the score then gives Eq. 27:

vt(x)=α˙tαtx−(β˙tβt−α˙tβt2αt)∇logpt(x)=−x1−t−t1−t∇logpt(x)

∇logpt(x)=−xt−1−ttvt(x)

  • The sampler (Eq. 28–29 = Eq. 8–9). Eq. 27 into the reverse SDE removes the score, and Euler–Maruyama makes every transition a Gaussian: mean the drift step, variance σt2|Δt|I. Time runs 1→0, so Δt<0 and the noise scale is |Δt|, the release's sqrt(-dt) term:

dxt=[vt(xt)+σt22t(xt+(1−t)vt(xt))]dt+σtdw

xt+Δt=xt+[vθ(xt,t)+σt22t(xt+(1−t)vθ(xt,t))]Δt+σtΔtϵ,ϵ~𝒩(0,I)

  • What the Gaussian buys. πθ(xt−1|xt,c)=𝒩(μθ,σt2|Δt|I), μθ the bracket above: the ratio rt(θ) is a quotient of two Gaussian densities and the KL a difference of means, both closed-form, so DDPO's machinery carries over — and the marginals stay the pretrained model's.

  • A KL that costs nothing (§4). Both policies are Gaussians of one variance, so the KL is a squared difference of predicted means — and, as the means differ only through the velocities, of predicted velocities: no second sampling pass, no estimator, no extra gradient variance:

DKL(πθ‖πref)=‖xt+Δt,θ−xt+Δt,ref‖22σt2Δt=Δt2(σt(1−t)2t+1σt)2‖vθ(xt,t)−vref(xt,t)‖2

  • The noise level is the exploration knob (§5.3). σt=at/(1−t), which keeps σt2/2t finite at t→0 and the noise nil at the data end (my reading): a=0.1 explores too thinly, 0.7 is best, past 1.0 nothing more, and too much noise destroys the samples and the reward with them.

  • The objective (Eq. 4–5). the advantage Ai=(Ri−meanR)/stdR over a group of G images for one prompt, the same scalar at every denoising step, against a per-step clipped ratio and the KL above. No value network appears anywhere, which is the reason GRPO and not PPO.

  • Denoising Reduction. Roll out at T=10 and still evaluate at T=40: over 4× faster on all three tasks with no loss in final reward, since low-quality trajectories carry the same ranking signal. Dropping to 5 is not reliably faster and sometimes slower, so ten is where it stops.

  • Group size decides stability (§5.3). G=24 trains through; at 12 and 6 the advantage estimate is noisy enough that both runs climb, peak and then collapse outright — a small group makes the denominator of Eq. 4 unreliable long before the numerator itself ever becomes so.

  • Experiments (§5). SD3.5-M on GenEval composition, OCR text rendering and PickScore, DrawBench quality watching for hacking: GenEval 0.63 → 0.95, past GPT-4o's 0.84, OCR 0.59 → 0.92, PickScore 21.72 → 23.31, ahead of SFT, Flow-DPO and their online variants.

  • KL is not early stopping (§5.2–5.3). Without it GenEval still reaches 0.95, but DrawBench aesthetics fall 5.39 → 4.93 and DeQA 4.07 → 2.77; on PickScore, quality holds and diversity collapses instead, different seeds landing on one style — which no early stop would have caught.

  • What a template teaches (§5.3, Tab. 3–4). Trained on 60 GenEval classes and counts of 2–4, it scores 0.90 on 20 unseen classes from 0.64, counts 5–6 objects at 0.48 (0.13), 12 at 0.12 (0.02), and lifts T2I-CompBench 2D-spatial 0.2850 → 0.5447 — relations, not prompts.

  • Why not a reward model (§1). RL routes so far tune on small prompt sets and, run against a learned scorer, drift into mode collapse and reward hacking; DPO drops both reward network and rollouts, learning from pairs (c,x0w≻x0l) a reference model drew and people ranked.

  • Bradley–Terry (Eq. 3–4). Preference is a sigmoid of a reward gap, pBT(x0w≻x0l∣c)=σ(r(c,x0w)−r(c,x0l)), and a reward net rϕ is fit by LBT(ϕ)=−𝔼[logσ(rϕ(c,x0w)−rϕ(c,x0l))] — plain binary classification on a static, human-labeled dataset, which is all a reward model ever learns.

  • RLHF, and its closed form (Eq. 5–7). Maximize 𝔼pθ[r(c,x0)]−βDKL[pθ(x0|c)‖pref(x0|c)]; the unique optimum is pθ*=prefexp(r/β)/Z(c) with Z(c)=∑x0prefexp(r/β), so r(c,x0)=βlog(pθ*(x0|c)/pref(x0|c))+βlogZ(c) — the reward is a function of the policy and nothing else.

  • DPO (Eq. 8). Put that reward into Eq. 4: Z(c) cancels across the pair, no reward model is ever fit and no sample is ever drawn, and the policy is trained directly by LDPO=−𝔼[logσ(βlog(pθ(x0w|c)/pref(x0w|c))−βlog(pθ(x0l|c)/pref(x0l|c)))] — the policy is its own reward model.

  • What diffusion breaks (§4, Eq. 9–10). Eq. 8 needs pθ(x0|c), which marginalizes each path x1:T ending at x0 — the one thing a diffusion model never gives. So define a chain reward R, r(c,x0)=𝔼pθ(x1:T|x0,c)[R(c,x0:T)], and bound the KL by the joint DKL[pθ(x0:T|c)‖pref(x0:T|c)].

  • The lift, line by line (S2, Eq. 17). Eq. 5 over β, sign flipped; each line below is one move — the ELBO bound, the reward swap of Eq. 9, completing the KL with Z(c) — and the last minimizer is read off: pθ*=prefexp(R/β)/Z(c), so R=βlog(pθ*/pref)+βlogZ(c), Eq. 7 on paths.

Concurrent with DDPO, it supplied the other half of diffusion RL — a KL to the pretrained model in the objective, the anchor later methods keep, tune, or argue away.

imagediffusionrl

The same multi-step MDP as DDPO, arrived at independently, and a KL to the pretrained model carried the whole way as an implicit reward.

  • The MDP, indexed backwards (Eq. 4). st=(z,xT−t), at=xT−t−1, πθ(at|st)=pθ(xT−t−1|xT−t,z), transitions (δz,δat), P0=(p(z),𝒩(0,I)), reward only at t=T−1 — the same mapping DDPO found, written so that MDP time runs forward while the denoising runs backward.

  • The objective and its gradient (Eq. 5–6). Minimize 𝔼p(z)𝔼pθ(x0|z)[−r(x0,z)]; Lemma 4.1 turns that into 𝔼[−r(x0,z)∑t∇θlogpθ(xt−1|xt,z)], REINFORCE over the T-step chain. The DDPM covariance stays frozen at Σ and only μθ is trained, which is what leaves the policy stochastic.

  • A KL you can actually compute (Lemma 4.2, Eq. 7). The divergence that matters is on the image, and that marginal is intractable — so bound it over all T steps by ∑t𝔼KL(pθ(xt−1|xt,z)‖ppre(xt−1|xt,z)), which makes every term a Gaussian KL with a closed form of its own.

  • The regularized objective (Eq. 8). 𝔼p(z)[α𝔼[−r(x0,z)]+β∑t𝔼KL(pθ‖ppre)] — α and β trade reward against drift, and the KL arrives as a second reward evaluated online, per update, on fresh samples, which is precisely the thing a supervised fine-tuning pass cannot do.

  • The gradient they actually use (Eq. 9) is not that objective's. 𝔼[−αr∑t∇θlogpθ+β∑t∇θKL(pθ‖ppre)] differentiates the KL's value but not the state distribution that the KL is measured under — a gap the main text flags plainly in the line right after the equation, not in the code.

  • The term they drop (App. A.3, Eq. 16). The KL sits under pθ(xt), the policy's own state distribution, so the product rule adds a second term, ∑t𝔼[KL(pθ‖ppre)∑s>t∇θlogpθ(xs−1|xs,z)]: each step then carries the KL of every step still ahead of it, dropped purely for its cost.

  • Reusing a round of samples (App. A.6, Eq. 25–26). Writing pold for the sampling policy, a ratio against it lets five gradient steps run off one round, and a PPO clip bounds the drift: 𝔼[−∑t(r−V)∇θclip(pθ/pold,1−ϵ,1+ϵ)], with V(xt,z) a learned value baseline that cuts variance.

  • KL for supervised fine-tuning too (Eq. 10–13). The baseline is 𝔼[−rlogpθ(x0|z)]; Lemma 4.3 bounds its KL-regularized form two ways — KL-D shifts the weight to r+γ, smoothing it toward uniform, and KL-O adds γ‖fpre−fθ‖2, pinning the denoising direction to the pretrained one.

  • Three ways online differs (§4.3). RL may search outside the pretrained support while SFT only imitates inside it; RL's KL is measured on new samples each update, SFT's on a frozen set; and RL queries the reward model off-distribution, so it inherits whatever that model generalizes.

  • Experiments (§5). SD v1.5, LoRA, ImageReward reward, one prompt per color, count, composition and location, judged by ImageReward, aesthetics, raters: RL beats SFT on all four, aesthetics intact; on 104 COCO / 183 DrawBench prompts it goes 0.22 → 0.55 / 0.13 → 0.58.

The multi-step MDP it defines is the formulation every later diffusion RL method either builds on or argues against.

imagediffusionrl

Read denoising as a multi-step decision process, and each sampler step becomes a Gaussian whose exact likelihood a policy gradient can use.

  • The objective training never optimizes (§4.1). A diffusion model is fit to a data distribution, but the goal is JDDRL(θ)=𝔼c,x0~pθ(x0|c)[r(x0,c)] for a black-box reward — and pθ(x0|c) is the one quantity a diffusion model cannot evaluate, since it marginalizes every denoising path.

  • Reward-weighted regression (§4.2). Collapse sampling to a one-step MDP (s=c, a=x0) and reweight the denoising loss by w=exp(βr)/Z — no heuristic, since the KL-constrained optimum is π*∝πoldexp(r/β) and fitting πθ to that optimum is reward-weighted maximum likelihood.

  • Except the likelihood is a bound (§4.2). That derivation needs logπθ itself, and a diffusion model's bound falls short of it by exactly KL(q(x1:T|x0)‖pθ(x1:T|x0)) — a divergence conditioned on that image, so the slack differs sharply from one sample to the next.

  • What the slack costs (App. E.2). The ratios exp(ri/β) reach the gradient scaled by an unknown factor, and the loss can fall by shrinking that slack instead of the likelihood — effort the reward pays for and the image never sees. RWR fed 16,384 samples a round never catches up.

  • Denoising as a multi-step MDP (§4.3). Choose what counts as an action and the rest of the mapping follows. Writing δy for a Dirac mass at y, the transitions here are deterministic and ρ0 starts every rollout at step T from pure noise, so all of the trajectories share one horizon.

  • The return is the reward (§4.3). Only the final step pays and nothing is discounted, so a whole trajectory returns exactly r(x0,c): maximizing JRL(π) in this MDP and maximizing JDDRL(θ) are literally the same problem, which is what licenses a policy gradient estimator here at all.

  • Why that buys exact likelihoods. Under a standard sampler the policy is the isotropic Gaussian pθ(xt−1|xt,c)=𝒩(μθ(xt,c,t),σt2I): per-step log-likelihoods and their gradients are exact, where the marginal over all of the many trajectories that could reach a given x0 never is.

  • The score function estimator, DDPOSF (§4.3). ∇θJDDRL=𝔼[∑t=0T∇θlogpθ(xt−1∣xt,c)r(x0,c)] — REINFORCE, the one terminal reward multiplying every step's score. The expectation runs over the current θ's own trajectories, so a round of data buys exactly one gradient step.

  • The importance-sampled estimator, DDPOIS (§4.3). ∇θJDDRL=𝔼[∑t=0T(pθ(xt−1∣xt,c)/pθold(xt−1∣xt,c))∇θlogpθ(xt−1∣xt,c)r(x0,c)] — the expectation now runs over θold's trajectories, so several updates fit one round; it decays as θ drifts, which the clipping bounds.

  • The clip range has to be tiny (App. D.5). 1e-4, four orders below what standard RL tasks use — the per-step Gaussians sit so close together that an ordinary trust region is no constraint at all, and rewards are normalized per prompt by a running mean and standard deviation.

  • Experiments (§6). SD v1.4 on four rewards — (in)compressibility over 398 animals, LAION aesthetics over 45, LLaVA alignment over 45 × 3 activities — reward per query: both DDPO variants beat both RWRs, IS ahead of SF, and alignment climbs on prompts the base never solved.

  • Every reward has a style tax (§6). Aesthetics turns photos into drawings, compressibility strips backgrounds, incompressibility adds noise, alignment goes cartoon-like unasked — the pretraining prior, or the VLM liking cartoons; and it transfers to unseen animals and objects.

The PyTorch release pins what the paper leaves to a table, and the choice that matters most is the one that makes the policy stochastic at all.

videoflowrl

A 182k pairwise video preference set and the reward model it trains, then DPO, RWR and reward guidance carried across to flow models.

  • The gap being filled (§1, §3.1). Existing video preference sets score pre-Sora generators — short clips, visible artifacts. This one annotates 12 modern models into 182k triplets on visual quality, motion quality and text alignment, three annotators each and a fourth when they disagree.

  • Pairwise beats pointwise (§3.2). The same annotators labeled the same videos both ways, so the comparison is clean: Bradley–Terry on pairs beats regression on 1–5 scores at every data fraction. Raters agree on which is better far more readily than on a number out of five.

  • Ties are data, not noise (§3.2). Plain Bradley–Terry throws tied pairs away; the with-ties extension models them, and the ties-included accuracy rises from 61.22 to 61.50 while ties-excluded goes 72.58 → 73.39 — the tied cases are what teach the model where its boundary lies.

  • The reward model (§3.2, §5.1). Qwen2-VL-2B, LoRA on the language model's linear layers with the vision encoder fully trained; a separate [VQ][MQ][TA] token per axis rather than one pooled last token; 2 fps at about 448×448, batch 32, lr 2e-6, two epochs, roughly 72 A800 hours.

  • Flow-DPO (Eq. 8–10). For rectified flow ‖ϵ*−ϵpred‖2=(1−t)2‖v*−vpred‖2, so Diffusion-DPO's noise-space loss becomes a velocity-space one otherwise unchanged — except that the KL coefficient picks up a schedule, βt=β(1−t)2, falling straight out of that same substitution.

  • And the schedule is the wrong one (§4.1, §5.2). βt=β(1−t)2 loosens the constraint exactly where the noise is highest, so the model aligns hardest where the signal is worst: VBench drops to 80.90 and text alignment to a 28.14 win rate. Holding βt constant gives 83.41 and 68.26.

  • Flow-RWR (§4.2). The same KL-regularized optimum pθ∝prefexp(r/β), read as an EM step and simplified to exp(r)‖ϵ−ϵθ‖2 with rewards normalized to zero mean and unit variance. It lands between SFT and Flow-DPO on every benchmark and beats neither of them.

  • Flow-NRG (§4.3). Guide at inference instead: v~t=vt−w(t/(1−t))∇r(xt,y) tilts the marginal toward exp(r)w, so weights over the three axes become a runtime dial. It needs a reward on noisy video, fitted by Bradley–Terry on noised pairs and read off the generator's own first 20 blocks.

  • Experiments (§5). The reward model on VideoGen-RewardBench (26.5k new pairs), accuracy with/without ties: 61.26 / 73.59 against VisionReward's 56.77 / 67.59; alignment by VBench and reward win rates — Flow-DPO 83.41 and 93.42 / 69.08 / 75.43 on VQ / MQ / TA.

  • LoRA is a requirement, not a shortcut (§5.2). Full-parameter fine-tuning degrades or collapses the model, on all three algorithms. The training set is relabeled by VideoReward before alignment, so scoring the result with VideoReward is at least self-consistent rather than circular.

  • Guidance weights are a runtime dial (§5.2, Tab. 6–7). VQ:MQ:TA at 0:0:1 lifts TA to 70.42, 0.5:0.5:0 lifts VQ / MQ to 86.43 / 93.23, no retraining; a reward head fit on clean latents fails on noisy ones (37.1 / 38.6 against 66.2 / 74.6), and predicting x0 first breaks the video.
