Online RL pools each prompt's rewards into one reward–endpoint covariance and regresses it at ODE states in two updates per rollout.
Insight. Examine an RL objective by the forces it applies at each state, not by its loss. Hidden terms explained a collapse and its cure.

  • NFT as one regression (§2, Eq. 1). NFT draws K endpoints x0 per prompt c from a frozen policy and maps rewards to r∈[0,1]. On xt=(1−t)x0+tϵ, it regresses vθ toward vtgt=aut+(1−a)vold, with ut=x0−ϵ and a=(2r−1)/β. An EMA moves the rollout policy.

  • The hidden consistency force (§3.1, Prop. 1, Eq. 3). Take q=𝔼[r∣st] and the shared guidance g=Cov(r,ut∣st) (Lemma 1, Eq. 2) at st=(xt,t,c). NFT's force is ℱNFT=2g+(2q−1)e, where e is the gap between the endpoints' forward velocity and the sampler's field.

  • EMA works by restoration (§3.2, Prop. 2, Eq. 4). With the drift d=vθ−vold and h=ut−vold, ℒNFT=𝔼[−2β(2r−1)⟨d,h⟩+β2‖d‖2]+const. The quadratic term pulls back to vold and is zero on-policy. So the EMA lag gives stability, but the decay schedule needs tuning per task.

  • Tilting as a covariance (§4.1, Lemma 2). For pτ∝poldexp(τR), ∂τ𝔼pτ[x0∣c]|τ=0=Cov(R,x0∣c) =Cov(R,ut∣c) at every noise level.

  • Aggregation-first guidance (§4.1, Eq. 5). Cov―=(1/K)∑i(Ri−R¯c)(x0(i)−x¯0,c), G(c)=Cov―/RMS(Cov―). Each visited state has the target vtgtART(st)=vθ(0)(st)+G(c), with the cached rollout velocity vθ(0). The RMS removes the reward scale, so no strength needs tuning.

  • Two updates per rollout (§4.2, Eq. 6). One step on each half of the rollout uses ℒ=wMSE(vθ,vtgtART), with MSE=D−1‖a−b‖2. The first step sees −∇vθℒ=(2w/D)G(c), and the second sees (2w/D)(G(c)−d(st)). Guidance dominates until the restoration −d grows with the drift.

  • KL proxy for an ODE (§4.3, Eq. 7, App. C.2). For ODE rollouts, a reference SDE step of mean μ(st,v)=xt+h[(1+ν2/2)v−ν2xt/(2(1−t))] and σref2=ν2ht/(1−t), with ν=0.8 and the step h=t−tnext, gives the per-coordinate KL m(st)=‖μ(st,vθ)−μ(st,vθ(0))‖2/(2Dσref2(t)).

  • Trust-region mask (§4.3, Eq. 8). w(st)=t[1−1{m(st)≥δ}1{⟨d,G−d⟩>0}] with a fixed δ=10−5 drops far states that move outward.

  • Experiments (§5). On SD3.5-M, FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1-I2V, FLUX gets GenEval 0.9684 (Flow-DPPO: 0.9183).

  • One pair per prompt is enough (Tab. 1). Winner/loser labels at K=2 give VQ 12.235 in 260.8 GPU hours. K=8 gives 12.092 in 883.3.

  • Reverse RL lowers VQ (Tab. 3). Flow-GRPO-Fast and DanceGRPO get VQ 3.149 and 3.922, below the base model's 5.195.

def shift_time(t, shift=3.0):
    return shift * t / (1 + (shift - 1) * t)

def guidance_direction(R, x0, eps=1e-5):
    x = x0.flatten(2)  # [prompts, K, D]
    cov = ((R - R.mean(1, keepdim=True))[..., None] * (x - x.mean(1, keepdim=True))).mean(1)  # Eq. 5
    G = cov / cov.square().mean(1, keepdim=True).sqrt().clamp(min=eps)  # Eq. 5. Equal rewards give G = 0.
    return G.view(x0.shape[0], *x0.shape[2:])

def reference_kl(d, t, t_next, nu=0.8):
    h = t - t_next
    var = nu ** 2 * h * t / (1 - t) if t < 1 else nu ** 2  # sigma_ref^2. The release takes nu^2 at t = 1.
    coef = h * (1 + nu ** 2 / 2) if t < 1 else h
    return coef ** 2 * d.square().flatten(1).mean(1) / (2 * var)  # Eq. 7

def train(
    model, prompts, reward_fn, optimizer,
    samples_per_prompt=24, num_steps=10, delta=1e-5, updates_per_rollout=2
):
    # 1. Roll out the endpoints of each prompt with the plain ODE sampler. Cache every state and velocity.
    P, K = len(prompts), samples_per_prompt
    c = prompts.repeat_interleave(K, 0)
    with no_grad():
        x0, states, v_old, t = sample(model, c, num_steps)
    R = reward_fn(x0, c).view(P, K)  # black-box scalar, no reward gradient

    # 2. Pool the rewards and endpoints of each prompt into one direction, and freeze the targets.
    G = guidance_direction(R, x0.view(P, K, *x0.shape[1:])).repeat_interleave(K, 0)
    v_tgt = v_old + G[:, None]  # Alg. 1 line 3, at every visited state

    # 3. Take one optimizer step on each disjoint part of the trajectories.
    for rows in torch.randperm(P * K).chunk(updates_per_rollout):
        for i in range(num_steps):
            v = model(states[rows, i], t[i], c[rows])
            d = v - v_old[rows, i]  # zero in the first update
            outward = (d * (G[rows] - d)).flatten(1).mean(1) > 0
            w = t[i] * (1 - ((reference_kl(d, t[i], t[i + 1]) >= delta) & outward).float())  # Eq. 8
            loss = (w * (v - v_tgt[rows, i]).square().flatten(1).mean(1)).mean()  # Eq. 6
            (loss / num_steps).backward()
        optimizer.step()
        optimizer.zero_grad()

def sample(model, c, num_steps=40):
    t = shift_time(linspace(1, 0, num_steps + 1))
    x = torch.randn(len(c), 16, 64, 64)
    states, vs = [], []
    for i in range(num_steps):
        v = model(x, t[i], c)
        states.append(x)
        vs.append(v)
        x = x + (t[i] - t[i + 1]) * v  # v points to data, as in the paper
    return x, torch.stack(states, 1), torch.stack(vs, 1), t

Each step of a pair of trajectories from one noise moves toward their reward-weighted image difference. The whole run is one action.
Insight. Diffusion RL may not need credit for each step. The difference of two paired runs gives a reward gradient cheap enough for raters.

  • Paired rollouts (§4). Two rollouts x0:T and x^0:T start from one noise ϵ. Their final images xT, x^T and the reward R give Δx=x^T−xT, ΔR=R(x^T)−R(xT). Training moves the velocities at all steps of both rollouts toward the reward-weighted image difference ΔRΔx.

  • Stochastic sampler (Alg. 1, App. B.1). Each step overshoots to t~i+1=ti+1/(1−γiti+1+γi) Eq. (4), with the noise fraction γi=0.0025. An Euler step gives x~i+1=xi+(t~i+1−ti)vi, and new noise ϵi returns it to ti+1: xi+1=(x~i+1+t~i+1γi2+2γiϵi)/(γit~i+1+1) Eq. (5).

  • Normalized difference (§4.3). To first order ΔR∝‖Δx‖, so ΔRΔx counts the distance twice. The signal is Δx―=Δx/(‖Δx‖RMS2+10−6).

  • Clipped objective (Alg. 2). PPO's ratio needs a likelihood. So the stored velocity vref gets the target vtarget=vref−Δx―. The current one vcur gives the proxy ratio ρ=exp(‖vtarget−vref‖2−‖vtarget−vcur‖2) in SPO's loss L=−ΔRρ−(|ΔR|/2ε)(ρ−1)2 with the clip range ε=0.03.

  • Experiments (§5). SD3.5-M with the reward PickScore + VLM alignment/10 gets OneIG 82.13, against 71.06 for Flow-GRPO.

  • The exact gradient does not help (Fig. 8 I–J). The true reward gradient gives 81.47. The gradient through the SDE gives 66.69.

def churn_step(x, v, t, s, gamma=0.0025):
    t_over = s / (1 - gamma * s + gamma)  # Eq. 4
    x_over = x + (t_over - t) * v  # Euler step beyond s
    noise = torch.randn_like(x)
    return (x_over + t_over * (gamma ** 2 + 2 * gamma) ** 0.5 * noise) / (gamma * t_over + 1)  # Eq. 5

def partner(a):
    return a.view(-1, 2, *a.shape[1:]).flip(1).reshape(a.shape)  # rows 2i and 2i + 1 swap

def reward(image, c, vlm_weight=0.1):
    r_vlm = 100 * torch.sigmoid(vlm_logit(image, c, "Yes") - vlm_logit(image, c, "No"))  # Qwen2.5-VL-7B
    return pickscore(image, c) + vlm_weight * r_vlm

def spo_loss(v_cur, v_ref, dR, dx, clip_range=0.03):
    v_target = (v_ref - dx).detach()  # t falls, so -dx moves x by +dx
    ps = -(v_target - v_cur).square().mean(dim=(1, 2, 3))  # means over pixels, not sums
    ps_ref = -(v_target - v_ref).square().mean(dim=(1, 2, 3))
    ratio = torch.exp(ps - ps_ref)
    return -dR * ratio - dR.abs() / (2 * clip_range) * (ratio - 1) ** 2  # release: + (ratio - 1) ** 2

def train(model, x_t, t, c, v_ref, dR, dx):
    # 1. Predict the current velocity at a stored state of either branch.
    v_cur = model(x_t, t, c)

    # 2. Calculate the SPO loss toward the reference velocity shifted by the pair's difference.
    loss = spo_loss(v_cur, v_ref, dR, dx).mean()

def sample(model, c, num_steps=40, gamma=0.0025):
    # 1. Draw one seed per pair, and churn it once per branch.
    eps = torch.randn(bs, 16, 64, 64).repeat_interleave(2, dim=0)
    x = (eps + (gamma ** 2 + 2 * gamma) ** 0.5 * torch.randn_like(eps)) / (gamma + 1)
    c = c.repeat_interleave(2, dim=0)

    # 2. Run both branches with the churned Euler sampler. The last step is deterministic.
    ts = sd3_timesteps(num_steps)  # 1 -> 0, shift 3
    xs, vs = [], []
    with no_grad():
        for i in range(num_steps):
            v = model(x, ts[i], c)
            xs.append(x)
            vs.append(v)
            x = churn_step(x, v, ts[i], ts[i + 1], gamma if i < num_steps - 1 else 0.0)

    # 3. Score the pair. The difference of each branch points from its partner to that branch.
    r = reward(vae_decode(x), c)
    dR = (r - partner(r)) / 2
    dx = (x - partner(x)) / 2
    dx = dx / (dx.square().mean(dim=(1, 2, 3), keepdim=True) + 1e-6)  # normalized by ||dx||^2_RMS
    return stack(xs, 1), stack(vs, 1), ts[:-1], c, dR, dx

DDPO is denoising score matching with noisy targets. AWM keeps the pretraining loss and weights each sample by its advantage.
Insight. Policy-gradient RL is pretraining with noisier targets. An advantage-weighted pretraining loss lets RL reuse the pretraining recipe.

  • DDPO as score matching (Thm 1, Eq. 6). A DDPO step with its Gaussian likelihood Eq. (4) targets 𝔼‖sθ(xt,t)−∇logp(xt∣xt−Δt)‖2. This is score matching on a noisy xt−Δt, not on the clean x0. It has the same minimizer but a target of higher variance (Lemma 1, Thm 2).

  • One-step MDP and its likelihood (§4, Eq. 10). The state is the prompt c, and the action is the clean image x0. The flow-matching surrogate −𝔼t[w(t)‖vθ(xt,t,c)−(ϵ−x0)‖2] gives logπθ(x0|c), with the uniform w(t)=1. It works better than the ELBO weight (1−t)/t Eq. (2).

  • GRPO on the surrogate (Eq. 9, 11–12). In GRPO's (πθ/πθold)Ai−βDKL Eq. (9), the ratio is exp(−𝔼t[w(t)(‖vθ−v‖2−‖vθold−v‖2)]) Eq. (11), with v=ϵ−x0. Both policies share t and ϵ to cut variance, with one ratio per image. The velocity-space KL is w(t)‖vθ−vθref‖2 Eq. (12).

  • The gradient (§4). On-policy, the update is −Ai∇θ𝔼t[w(t)‖vθ−(ϵ−x0)‖2], a scaled pretraining gradient. So any sampler can roll out.

  • Experiments (§5). On SD3.5-M, AWM reaches GenEval 0.95 with 8× less compute than Flow-GRPO, and OCR 0.89 with 23.6× less.

  • The t prior matters (§5.2). Uniform and the sampler's grid score equally. Logit-normal, pretraining's default, lags and then degrades.

The training and sampling code. Follow Algorithm 1 of the paper.

def shift_time(t, shift=3.0):
    return shift * t / (1 + (shift - 1) * t)

def awm_log_prob(v, x0, noise, t, ghuber_power=0.5):
    mse = (v - (noise - x0)).square().flatten(1).mean(1)  # Eq. 10, the flow-matching loss
    return -((mse + 1e-10) ** ghuber_power - 1e-10 ** ghuber_power) * t / ghuber_power  # generalized Huber, times t

def sample_train_steps(num_steps=14, train_timesteps=6, timestep_fraction=0.9):
    edges = linspace(1, int(num_steps * timestep_fraction), train_timesteps).long()
    idx = edges[:-1] + (torch.rand(train_timesteps - 1) * (edges[1:] - edges[:-1])).long()  # one per bin
    return cat([torch.zeros(1).long(), idx])  # the sampler's first step always trains

def group_advantage(rewards, num_image_per_prompt=24, adv_clip_max=5.0, advantage_max=1.0):
    r = rewards.view(-1, num_image_per_prompt)
    adv = (r - r.mean(1, keepdim=True)) / (rewards.std(unbiased=False) + 1e-4)  # per-prompt mean, global std
    return adv.flatten().clamp(-adv_clip_max, adv_clip_max) / adv_clip_max * advantage_max

def train(
    model, ref_model, ema_model, prompts, reward_fn, optimizer, step,
    num_image_per_prompt=24, beta=0.003, ema_beta=1.0, base_kl_cfg_scale=4.5, clip_range=1.0,
    kl_ema_decay=0.3, kl_ema_decay_rate=0.001
):
    # 1. Roll out images with any sampler and score them.
    c = prompts.repeat_interleave(num_image_per_prompt, 0)
    with no_grad():
        x0, t_grid = sample(model, c)
    adv = group_advantage(reward_fn(x0, c), num_image_per_prompt)

    # 2. Add noise to each image once per training timestep.
    for t in t_grid[sample_train_steps()]:
        noise = torch.randn_like(x0)  # shared by all images of a prompt
        x_t = (1 - t) * x0 + t * noise

        # 3. Calculate the ratio of the matching-loss likelihoods, one ratio per image.
        v = model(x_t, t, c)
        log_p = awm_log_prob(v, x0, noise, t)
        ratio = torch.exp(log_p - log_p.detach())  # Eq. 11. On-policy, the old policy is the current one.
        policy_loss = torch.maximum(-adv * ratio, -adv * ratio.clamp(1 - clip_range, 1 + clip_range)).mean()

        # 4. Add the velocity-space KL to the guided base model and to the EMA copy.
        with no_grad():
            v_ref_u, v_ref_c = ref_model(x_t, t, null), ref_model(x_t, t, c)
            v_ref = v_ref_u + base_kl_cfg_scale * (v_ref_c - v_ref_u)
            v_ema = ema_model(x_t, t, c)
        kl = (v - v_ref).square().flatten(1).mean(1).mean()  # Eq. 12, w(t) = 1
        ema_kl = (v - v_ema).square().flatten(1).mean(1).mean()
        (policy_loss + beta * kl + ema_beta * ema_kl).backward()
    optimizer.step()

    # 5. Move the EMA copy toward the policy. Its decay increases linearly.
    decay = min(kl_ema_decay, kl_ema_decay_rate * step)
    for p_ema, p in zip(ema_model.parameters(), model.parameters()):
        p_ema.data = decay * p_ema.data + (1 - decay) * p.data

Online RL on the forward process: one flow-matching loss learns the positive and negative policies that the reward defines on the rollouts.
Insight. RL on the forward process needs no likelihoods or SDE samplers. Unlike in language models, negative samples are necessary.

  • Positive and negative policies (§3.1). The reward r∈[0,1] is p(o=1∣x0,c), the probability that the image x0 for the prompt c is good. Bayes' rule on the old policy πold gives π+(x0∣c)=p(x0∣o=1,c)=p(o=1∣x0,c)πold(x0∣c)/p(o=1∣c)=rπold/𝔼πoldr and π−∝(1−r)πold.

  • Implicit policies (§3.2). The trained network vθ and the frozen old-policy network vold give two implicit policies, vθ+=(1−β)vold+βvθ, vθ−=(1+β)vold−βvθ, symmetric about vold. β is the weight of vθ (β=1), so a change of vθ+ is a 1/β times larger change of vθ.

  • Reinforcement guidance (Eq. 3–4). As in CFG, the target is v*=vold+(1/β)Δ Eq. (3), where Δ is the improvement direction and 1/β is its strength. v± are the velocities of π±, and Δ=α(v+−vold)=(1−α)(vold−v−) Eq. (4), with α=p(o=1∣xt) for the noised image xt.

  • One loss (Eq. 5–6). Flow matching on π+ weights the rollouts by r: 𝔼x0~π+‖vθ+−v‖2=𝔼x0~πold[r‖vθ+−v‖2]/𝔼r, where v=ϵ−x0 is the target velocity and ϵ is the noise. The same step on π− gives the loss ℒ=𝔼[r‖vθ+−v‖2+(1−r)‖vθ−−v‖2], with the optimum vold+(2/β)Δ Eq. (6).

  • Reward to probability (Alg. 1). The raw reward rraw becomes r=1/2+1/2clip[(rraw−𝔼πoldrraw)/Zc,−1,1], where the mean is over the group of the prompt and Zc is 5 times the global standard deviation of the reward. Thus r is group-relative and stays a probability.

  • Soft update of the old policy (§3.3). The old policy is an EMA copy of the model, θold←ηiθold+(1−ηi)θ, with ηi=min(0.001i,0.5) at iteration i. A small ηi learns fast but can be unstable, and a large ηi is stable but slow. The training loss needs no importance ratio.

  • Loss weighting (§3.3). A self-normalized x0 regression replaces the time weight w(t) of the flow-matching loss, so no time weight needs manual tuning: ‖xθ−x0‖2/sg(mean|xθ−x0|), where xθ=xt−tvθ is the predicted clean image and sg is the stop-gradient operator.

  • Any solver, no CFG (§3.2). Training needs only clean images, so any solver can make the rollouts. The policy starts without CFG.

  • Experiments (§4). SD3.5-M without CFG reaches GenEval 0.94, above SD3.5-L with CFG, and is 3–25× faster than Flow-GRPO.

  • Positive samples alone collapse (§4.4). Without v−, the reward collapses. But language models train well on positive samples alone.

1. Prepare the group data for one epoch of training.

def prepare_data(dataset, epoch, seed=42, num_batches=16, num_groups=3, group_size=24):
    prompts = []
    for i in range(num_batches):
        # Make sure the sample order is the same across ranks.
        g = torch.Generator()
        g.manual_seed(seed + epoch * num_batches + i)

        # Pick the first num_groups samples, for example ["dog", "sun", "car"].
        indices = torch.randperm(len(dataset), generator=g)[:num_groups]

        # Repeat each sample to make the groups, for example ["dog", "dog", "sun", "sun", "car", "car"].
        repeated = [idx for idx in indices.tolist() for _ in range(group_size)]

        # Shuffle the samples, so that each batch contains samples from different groups.
        order = torch.randperm(len(repeated), generator=g).tolist()
        prompts += [dataset[repeated[k]] for k in order]  # one global batch of num_groups whole groups
    return prompts

2. Calculate the group advantages.

def group_advantages(prompts, rewards):
    prompts = np.array(prompts)
    adv = np.zeros_like(rewards)
    std = rewards.std() + 1e-4  # one std over all groups
    for p in np.unique(prompts):
        group = rewards[prompts == p]
        adv[prompts == p] = (group - group.mean()) / std  # Alg. 1 line 4
    return adv

3. Roll out the prompts.

def sample(model, c, num_steps=10, solver="dpm2", resolution=512):
    x = torch.randn(len(c), 16, resolution // 8, resolution // 8)
    sigmas = sd3_sigmas(num_steps)  # 1 -> 0, shift 3
    x0_preds = []
    for i in range(num_steps):
        v = model(x, (sigmas[i] * 1000).expand(len(c)), c)  # no CFG
        if solver == "flow":
            x = x + (sigmas[i + 1] - sigmas[i]) * v  # Euler
        else:
            x0_preds.append(x - sigmas[i] * v)
            x = dpm2_step(x0_preds, x, sigmas, i)  # 2nd-order multistep DPM-Solver
    return x, sigmas[:-1] * 1000

def rollout(old_model, prompts, reward_fn, batch_size=9, num_steps=10):
    # Sample each batch of prompts with the old policy. Keep only the clean latents and calculate their rewards.
    batches = []
    for k in range(0, len(prompts), batch_size):
        p = prompts[k:k + batch_size]
        c = encode_prompt(p)
        with no_grad():
            x0, timesteps = sample(old_model, c, num_steps)
        order = torch.stack([torch.randperm(num_steps) for _ in p])  # each image's own timestep order
        batches.append({
            "prompt_embeds": c,
            "latents": x0,
            "timesteps": timesteps.repeat(len(p), 1).gather(1, order),
            "rewards": reward_fn(vae_decode(x0), p),
        })
    samples = {k: torch.cat([b[k] for b in batches]) for k in batches[0]}

    # Calculate the group-relative advantages over all samples of the epoch.
    samples["advantages"] = torch.as_tensor(group_advantages(prompts, samples.pop("rewards").numpy()))
    return samples

def adaptive_x0_loss(x0_pred, x0, eps=1e-5):
    weight = (x0_pred - x0).abs().flatten(1).mean(1).clip(min=eps).detach()  # each sample's mean error

    # 2. Predict the velocities of the old, trained, and reference policies.
    with no_grad():

    v = model(x_t, timesteps, c)

    adv = torch.clamp(batch["advantages"], -adv_clip_max, adv_clip_max)
    r = adv / adv_clip_max / 2 + 0.5  # Alg. 1 line 5

    # 4. Calculate the loss of the implicit positive and negative policies.
    v_pos = beta * v + (1 - beta) * v_old  # see paper Eq. 5; Alg. 1 line 10
    v_neg = (1 + beta) * v_old - beta * v  # see paper Eq. 5; Alg. 1 line 11
    loss_pos = adaptive_x0_loss(x_t - t * v_pos, x0)
    loss_neg = adaptive_x0_loss(x_t - t * v_neg, x0)
    policy_loss = ((r * loss_pos + (1 - r) * loss_neg) / beta * adv_clip_max).mean()  # Eq. 5

    # 5. Add the KL to the reference policy as the squared velocity difference.
    kl = (v - v_ref).square().flatten(1).mean(1)
    return policy_loss + kl_beta * kl.mean()

def soft_update(old_model, model, step, eta_rate=0.001, eta_max=0.5, eta_delay=0):
    eta = 0.0 if step < eta_delay else min((step - eta_delay) * eta_rate, eta_max)
    for p_old, p in zip(old_model.parameters(), model.parameters()):
        p_old.data = eta * p_old.data + (1 - eta) * p.data  # Alg. 1 line 14

def train_epoch(
    model, old_model, optimizer, dataset, reward_fn, epoch, step,
    batch_size=9, updates_per_epoch=1, max_grad_norm=1.0, num_steps=10, timestep_fraction=0.99
):
    # Roll out the prompts of one epoch with the old policy, the EMA of the model.
    prompts = prepare_data(dataset, epoch)
    samples = rollout(old_model, prompts, reward_fn, batch_size, num_steps)

    # Keep all samples, because a zero advantage also trains (r = 0.5). Shuffle them and divide them into batches.
    num_batches = len(prompts) // batch_size
    perm = torch.randperm(len(samples["advantages"]))
    batches = [
        {k: v[perm[b * batch_size:(b + 1) * batch_size]] for k, v in samples.items()} for b in range(num_batches)
    ]

    # Accumulate the loss of each trained timestep, and update the model updates_per_epoch times.
    accu, T = num_batches // updates_per_epoch, int(num_steps * timestep_fraction)
    for b, batch in enumerate(batches):
        for j in range(T):
            (train(model, old_model, batch, j) / (accu * T)).backward()
        if (b + 1) % accu == 0:
            clip_grad_norm_(model.parameters(), max_grad_norm)
            optimizer.step()
            optimizer.zero_grad()
            step += 1

    soft_update(old_model, model, step)
    return step

def shift_time(t, shift=3.0):
    return shift * t / (1 + (shift - 1) * t)

def sde_step(v, z, sigma, sigma_next, z_next=None, eta=0.3):
    x0 = z - sigma * v  # the step's clean prediction
    score = -(z - (1 - sigma) * x0) / sigma ** 2  # closed-form Gaussian score
    mean = z + (sigma_next - sigma) * (v - 0.5 * eta ** 2 * score)  # Eq. 8
    std = eta * math.sqrt(sigma - sigma_next)
    if z_next is None:
        z_next = mean + std * torch.randn_like(z)
    log_prob = -(z_next.detach() - mean) ** 2 / (2 * std ** 2) - math.log(std) - math.log(math.sqrt(2 * math.pi))
    return z_next, x0, log_prob.flatten(1).mean(1)

def group_advantage(rewards, num_generations=12):
    r = rewards.view(-1, num_generations)
    return ((r - r.mean(1, keepdim=True)) / (r.std(1, keepdim=True) + 1e-8)).flatten()  # Eq. 10

def train(
    model, prompts, reward_fns, optimizer,
    num_generations=12, sampling_steps=16, timestep_fraction=0.6, clip_range=1e-4, adv_clip_max=5.0
):
    # 1. Roll out a group per prompt from one shared noise.
    c = prompts.repeat_interleave(num_generations, 0)
    noise = torch.randn(len(prompts), 16, 90, 90).repeat_interleave(num_generations, 0)  # one noise per group
    with no_grad():
        x0, zs, old_log_probs, sigmas = sample(model, c, noise, sampling_steps)

    # 2. Sum the group advantages of every reward model.
    adv = sum(group_advantage(reward_fn(x0, c), num_generations) for reward_fn in reward_fns)  # Alg. 1 line 8
    adv = adv.clamp(-adv_clip_max, adv_clip_max)

    # 3. Keep a random subset of the steps. The last step is never trained.
    steps = torch.randperm(sampling_steps - 1)[:int((sampling_steps - 1) * timestep_fraction)]

    # 4. Calculate the log-prob of each kept step again and take the clipped-ratio loss.
    for i in steps:
        v = model(zs[:, i], sigmas[i], c)
        _, _, log_prob = sde_step(v, zs[:, i], sigmas[i], sigmas[i + 1], z_next=zs[:, i + 1])
        ratio = torch.exp(log_prob - old_log_probs[:, i])
        loss = torch.maximum(-adv * ratio, -adv * ratio.clamp(1 - clip_range, 1 + clip_range)).mean()  # Eq. 9
        (loss / len(steps)).backward()
    optimizer.step()

def prepare_data(dataset, epoch, seed=42, num_batches=4, num_groups=12, group_size=24):
    prompts = []
    for i in range(num_batches):
        # Make sure the sample order is the same across ranks.
        g = torch.Generator()
        g.manual_seed(seed + epoch * num_batches + i)

        # Pick the first num_groups samples, for example ["dog", "sun", "car"].
        indices = torch.randperm(len(dataset), generator=g)[:num_groups]

        # Repeat each sample to make the groups, for example ["dog", "dog", "sun", "sun", "car", "car"].
        repeated = [idx for idx in indices.tolist() for _ in range(group_size)]

        # Shuffle the samples, so that each batch contains samples from different groups.
        order = torch.randperm(len(repeated), generator=g).tolist()
        prompts += [dataset[repeated[k]] for k in order]  # one global batch of num_groups whole groups
    return prompts

def group_advantages(prompts, rewards):
    prompts = np.array(prompts)
    adv = np.zeros_like(rewards)
    std = rewards.std() + 1e-4  # one std over all groups
    for p in np.unique(prompts):
        group = rewards[prompts == p]
        adv[prompts == p] = (group - group.mean()) / std  # Eq. 4
    return adv

def sde_step(v, x, sigma, sigma_prev, sigma_max, noise_level=0.7, x_prev=None):
    dt = sigma_prev - sigma  # dt < 0: sigma runs 1 -> 0
    std_t = noise_level * torch.sqrt(sigma / (1 - torch.where(sigma == 1, sigma_max, sigma)))  # a sqrt(t / (1 - t))
    mean = x + (v + std_t ** 2 / (2 * sigma) * (x + (1 - sigma) * v)) * dt  # Eq. 9
    std = std_t * torch.sqrt(-dt)
    if x_prev is None:
        x_prev = mean + std * torch.randn_like(x)
    log_prob = -(x_prev.detach() - mean) ** 2 / (2 * std ** 2) - torch.log(std) - math.log(math.sqrt(2 * math.pi))
    return x_prev, log_prob.mean(dim=(1, 2, 3)), mean, std_t

def guided_v(model, x, t, c, guidance_scale=4.5):
    v_u, v_c = model(cat([x, x]), cat([t, t]), cat([null, c])).chunk(2)
    return v_u + guidance_scale * (v_c - v_u)

def rollout(model, prompts, reward_fn, batch_size=9, num_steps=10, timestep_fraction=0.99):
    # Sample each batch of prompts with the SDE, and calculate the rewards of its images.
    T = int(num_steps * timestep_fraction)  # the last step is never trained
    batches = []
    for k in range(0, len(prompts), batch_size):

        batches.append({

            "latents": latents[:, :T],
            "next_latents": latents[:, 1:T + 1],
            "steps": torch.arange(T).repeat(len(p), 1),

    samples = {k: torch.cat([b[k] for b in batches]) for k in batches[0]}

    # Calculate the group-relative advantages over all samples of the epoch.
    samples["advantages"] = torch.as_tensor(group_advantages(prompts, samples.pop("rewards").numpy()))
    return samples, sigmas

def train(model, batch, j, sigmas, noise_level=0.7, clip_range=1e-4, adv_clip_max=5.0, beta=0.04):
    # 1. Get the stored step j of each sample.
    x, x_prev = batch["latents"][:, j], batch["next_latents"][:, j]
    c, t = batch["prompt_embeds"], batch["timesteps"][:, j]
    i = batch["steps"][:, j].view(-1, 1, 1, 1)
    sigma, sigma_prev = sigmas[i], sigmas[i + 1]

    # 2. Calculate the log prob of the stored next latent again under the current policy.
    v = guided_v(model, x, t, c)
    _, log_prob, mean, std_t = sde_step(v, x, sigma, sigma_prev, sigmas[1], noise_level, x_prev=x_prev)

    # 3. Calculate the clipped policy loss.
    adv = torch.clamp(batch["advantages"], -adv_clip_max, adv_clip_max)
    ratio = torch.exp(log_prob - batch["log_probs"][:, j])
    clipped = torch.clamp(ratio, 1 - clip_range, 1 + clip_range)
    policy_loss = torch.maximum(-adv * ratio, -adv * clipped).mean()  # Eq. 5

    # 4. Add the closed-form KL to the reference policy, the base model with LoRA off.
    with no_grad(), model.disable_adapter():
        v_ref = guided_v(model, x, t, c)
        _, _, mean_ref, _ = sde_step(v_ref, x, sigma, sigma_prev, sigmas[1], noise_level, x_prev=x_prev)
    kl = ((mean - mean_ref) ** 2).mean(dim=(1, 2, 3)) / (2 * std_t.flatten() ** 2)
    return policy_loss + beta * kl.mean()  # Eq. 5

Concurrent with DDPO, it added the second half of diffusion RL: a KL to the pretrained model in the objective. Later methods keep it, tune it or argue against it.

imagediffusionrl

DPOK finds the same multi-step MDP as DDPO independently and adds a KL to the pretrained model at each step as an implicit reward.
Insight. Online reward fine-tuning needs a KL to the pretrained model. The gradient path of the KL, not its weight, decides whether it helps.

  • The MDP (Eq. 4). For a prompt z, st=(z,xT−t), at=xT−t−1, πθ(at|st)=pθ(xT−t−1|xT−t,z), and the reward is only at t=T−1.

  • REINFORCE (Eq. 5–6). The gradient of 𝔼[−r(x0,z)] Eq. (5) is 𝔼[−r(x0,z)∑t∇θlogpθ(xt−1|xt,z)] Eq. (6) for a fixed DDPM covariance.

  • KL-regularized objective (Lemma 4.2, Eq. 8). DPOK minimizes 𝔼z[α𝔼[−r(x0,z)]+β∑t𝔼KL(pθ‖ppre)], with the pretrained model ppre and β=0.01. The per-step KL sum bounds the image KL Eq. (7). One shared variance makes each KL a squared gap of noise predictions.

  • Gradient used (Eq. 9, App. A.3). It is the REINFORCE term plus a direct β∑t∇θKL, without the KL term through the states, Eq. (16).

  • Value baseline (App. A.5, Eq. 24). A baseline V=𝔼[r|xt] Eq. (23) keeps the gradient unbiased. V^ minimizes 𝔼[(r(x0,z)−V^(xt,z))2].

  • Sample reuse (App. A.6, Eq. 26). An importance ratio against the sampling policy pold Eq. (25) lets five gradient steps share one sampling step. A PPO-style clip bounds the drift of the policy: 𝔼[−∑t(r−V)∇θclip(pθ/pold,1−ϵ,1+ϵ)], with the clip hyperparameter ϵ=10−4.

  • Experiments (§5). On SD v1.5, RL is better than supervised fine-tuning on all four tasks: COCO ImageReward goes 0.22 → 0.55.

  • Whiskey to flowers (§5.4). The base model draws whiskey for "Four roses", and RL draws flowers (ImageReward −0.52 → 1.12).

def ddim_step(eps, x_t, t, t_prev, eta=1.0, x_prev=None):
    a_t, a_prev = alphas_cumprod[t], alphas_cumprod[t_prev]
    x_0 = (x_t - (1 - a_t).sqrt() * eps) / a_t.sqrt()
    std = eta * ((1 - a_prev) / (1 - a_t) * (1 - a_t / a_prev)).sqrt()  # eta = 1: the DDPM variance, kept fixed
    mean = a_prev.sqrt() * x_0 + (1 - a_prev - std ** 2).sqrt() * eps
    if x_prev is None:
        x_prev = mean + std * torch.randn_like(x_t)
    log_prob = -(x_prev.detach() - mean) ** 2 / (2 * std ** 2) - std.log() - math.log(math.sqrt(2 * math.pi))
    return x_prev, log_prob.mean(dim=(1, 2, 3))  # mean over latent dims, not a sum

def train(
    unet, unet_pre, value_fn, buffer,
    reward_weight=100.0, kl_weight=0.01, ratio_clip=1e-4, p_batch_size=2, v_batch_size=256
):
    # 1. Fit the value baseline to the final reward on a large buffer batch.
    x_v, _, t_v, _, c_v, r_v, _ = buffer.sample(v_batch_size)
    value_loss = (value_fn(x_v, c_v, t_v) - r_v).square().mean()  # Eq. 24

    # 2. Draw stored transitions and calculate their log-probability again under the current policy.
    x_t, x_prev, t, t_prev, c, r, log_prob_old = buffer.sample(p_batch_size)
    eps = guided_eps(unet, x_t, t, c)
    _, log_prob = ddim_step(eps, x_t, t, t_prev, x_prev=x_prev)

    # 3. Weight the clamped ratio by the advantage.
    adv = (r - value_fn(x_t, c, t)).detach()
    ratio = torch.clamp(torch.exp(log_prob - log_prob_old), 1 - ratio_clip, 1 + ratio_clip)  # Eq. 26
    policy_loss = (-reward_weight * adv * ratio).mean()

    # 4. Add the KL to the pretrained model as the squared difference of the two noise predictions.
    with no_grad():
        eps_pre = guided_eps(unet_pre, x_t, t, c)
    loss = policy_loss + kl_weight * (eps - eps_pre).square().mean()  # Eq. 8

The multi-step MDP that it defines is the formulation that every later diffusion RL method builds on or argues against.

imagediffusionrl

DDPO reads denoising as a multi-step decision process. Each sampler step is a Gaussian, and a policy gradient uses its exact likelihood.
Insight. One action per denoising step made online RL possible for diffusion. Later methods keep this per-step view or try to remove it.

  • The objective (§4.1). Maximize JDDRL(θ)=𝔼c,x0~pθ(x0|c)[r(x0,c)] for a black-box reward r and a prompt c. pθ(x0|c) is intractable.

  • Reward-weighted regression, RWR (§4.2). A one-step MDP scales the diffusion loss by w=exp(βr)/Z. It only bounds logpθ(x0|c).

  • Multi-step MDP (§4.3). Each step is an action, and δy is a Dirac mass at y. Only the last step gets a reward, so JRL(π)=JDDRL(θ):

  • Exact likelihoods (Eq. 2). Under a standard sampler the policy is pθ(xt−1|xt,c)=𝒩(μθ(xt,c,t),σt2I), so its log-likelihoods are exact.

  • CFG training (App. E.1, Eq. 3). Training uses the guided ϵ~θ(xt,t,c)=wϵθ(xt,t,c)+(1−w)ϵθ(xt,t) with w=5, as in the sampler.

  • Importance-sampled estimator (§4.3). REINFORCE, DDPOSF, allows one gradient step per round. DDPOIS reuses θold's trajectories for four updates per round: 𝔼[∑t(pθ(xt−1|xt,c)/pθold(xt−1|xt,c))∇θlogpθ(xt−1|xt,c)r(x0,c)]. It is accurate while pθ stays close to pθold.

  • Clipped objective (App. D.1, D.3). PPO's clipped surrogate on the ratio ρt of each step is min(ρtA,clip(ρt,1−ϵ,1+ϵ)A), with ϵ=10−4, far below the usual RL clip ranges. The advantage A is the reward normalized by a running mean and std per prompt, like a value baseline.

  • Experiments (§6). On SD v1.4 with three rewards, DDPO is better than RWR per reward query, and DDPOIS is better than DDPOSF.

  • Style changes (§6). Aesthetics turns photos into drawings, compressibility removes backgrounds, and alignment gives cartoons.

def advantages(prompts, rewards, history, buffer_size=16, min_count=16):
    adv = torch.empty_like(rewards)
    for p in set(prompts):
        idx = [i for i, q in enumerate(prompts) if q == p]
        history[p] = (history.get(p, []) + rewards[idx].tolist())[-buffer_size:]
        ref = torch.tensor(history[p]) if len(history[p]) >= min_count else rewards
        adv[idx] = (rewards[idx] - ref.mean()) / (ref.std(unbiased=False) + 1e-6)  # per-prompt running stats
    return adv

def train(unet, x_t, x_prev, t, t_prev, c, log_prob_old, adv, adv_clip_max=5.0, clip_range=1e-4):
    # 1. Calculate the log-probability of a stored step again under the current policy.
    _, log_prob = ddim_step(guided_eps(unet, x_t, t, c), x_t, t, t_prev, x_prev=x_prev)

    adv = torch.clamp(adv, -adv_clip_max, adv_clip_max)
    ratio = torch.exp(log_prob - log_prob_old)
    loss = torch.maximum(-adv * ratio, -adv * torch.clamp(ratio, 1 - clip_range, 1 + clip_range)).mean()

Insight. In video RL, the reward model is the bottleneck. Reward guidance works at inference with no training, so it is a cheap first try.

  • The dataset (§3.1). 182k triplets from 12 generators have labels for visual quality (VQ), motion quality (MQ) and text alignment (TA).

  • Bradley–Terry with ties (§3.2, Eq. 3–6). For a prompt y, two videos A, B and their rewards rA, rB, P(A≻B|y)=erA/(erA+θerB) Eq. (4) and P(A=B|y)=(θ2−1)erAerB/((erA+θerB)(θerA+erB)) Eq. (3). Their NLL Eq. (6), with the tie parameter θ=5.0, also trains on ties.

  • The reward model (§3.2). Qwen2-VL-2B has LoRA on the LLM, a fully tuned vision encoder and one [VQ][MQ][TA] token per axis.

  • Flow-DPO (Eq. 8–10). Because ‖ϵ*−ϵpred‖2=(1−t)2‖v*−vpred‖2 Eq. (9), Diffusion-DPO becomes −𝔼logσ(−(βt/2)[(‖vw−vθ(xtw)‖2−‖vw−vref(xtw)‖2)−(‖vl−vθ(xtl)‖2−‖vl−vref(xtl)‖2)]). A fixed β=500 replaces its βt=β(1−t)2.

  • Noisy reward (§4.3, Eq. 16–17). Guidance needs r on the noisy xt. If one noise on both videos keeps the preference Eq. (16), −𝔼logσ(rθ(xtw,t)−rθ(xtl,t)) Eq. (17) fits it. The model is three fully connected heads on the generator's first 20 transformer blocks.

  • Experiments (§5). On VideoGen-RewardBench, accuracy with / without ties is 61.26 / 73.59, against VisionReward's 56.77 / 67.59.

  • LoRA is necessary (§5.2). Full-parameter fine-tuning degrades or collapses the generator, so all alignment runs tune only LoRA.

def score(vlm, rm_head, inputs, special_ids):
    logits = rm_head(vlm(inputs).last_hidden_state)  # one linear head, three outputs, no bias
    at_tokens = torch.isin(inputs.input_ids, special_ids)  # the [VQ], [MQ], [TA] positions
    return logits[at_tokens].view(-1, 3, 3).diagonal(dim1=1, dim2=2)  # axis k read at its own token

def btt_loss(r_a, r_b, label, theta=5.0):
    # label per axis: 1 if A wins, -1 if B wins, 0 for a tie
    r_w, r_l = where(label == 1, r_a, r_b), where(label == 1, r_b, r_a)
    log_theta = math.log(theta)
    win = -F.logsigmoid(r_w - r_l - log_theta)  # Eq. 4
    tie = (-F.logsigmoid(r_a - r_b - log_theta) - F.logsigmoid(r_b - r_a - log_theta)
           - math.log(theta ** 2 - 1))  # Eq. 3
    return where(label == 0, tie, win).mean()  # Eq. 6

def train(vlm, rm_head, inputs_a, inputs_b, label, special_ids):
    # 1. Score both videos on the three axes.
    r_a = score(vlm, rm_head, inputs_a, special_ids)
    r_b = score(vlm, rm_head, inputs_b, special_ids)

    # 2. Calculate the Bradley-Terry-with-ties loss on each axis.
    loss = btt_loss(r_a, r_b, label)

def flow_dpo(model, ref_model, x_w, x_l, c, beta=500.0):
    # 1. Add one noise to both videos at one timestep.
    t = torch.rand(len(x_w))
    noise = torch.randn_like(x_w)
    xw_t, xl_t = (1 - t) * x_w + t * noise, (1 - t) * x_l + t * noise

    # 2. Compare the velocity errors of the policy and the reference on the pair.
    err = lambda m, x_t, x0: (m(x_t, c, t) - (noise - x0)).pow(2).sum()
    with no_grad():
        ref_w, ref_l = err(ref_model, xw_t, x_w), err(ref_model, xl_t, x_l)
    inside = -0.5 * beta * (
        (err(model, xw_t, x_w) - ref_w) - (err(model, xl_t, x_l) - ref_l))  # Eq. 10, constant beta
    loss = -F.logsigmoid(inside)

def noisy_reward_loss(noisy_reward, x_w, x_l, c):
    t = torch.rand(len(x_w))
    noise = torch.randn_like(x_w)  # one noise for both videos keeps the preference
    r_w = noisy_reward((1 - t) * x_w + t * noise, c, t)  # Eq. 16
    r_l = noisy_reward((1 - t) * x_l + t * noise, c, t)
    return -F.logsigmoid(r_w - r_l).mean()  # Eq. 17

def sample(model, noisy_reward, z, c, timesteps, w, reward_weight):
    for i in range(len(timesteps) - 1):
        t = timesteps[i]
        v = model(z, c, t)
        z = z.detach().requires_grad_(True)
        r = (noisy_reward(z, c, t) * reward_weight).sum()  # weights over VQ, MQ, TA
        v = v - w * t / (1 - t) * torch.autograd.grad(r, z)[0]  # Eq. 15
        z = z - (t - timesteps[i + 1]) * v
    return z

imagediffusionrlunread

A text-to-image reward model learns from 137k expert comparisons, and ReFL tunes diffusion models by backpropagation of that reward.
Insight. A differentiable reward needs no likelihood. Score the model's one-step prediction late in sampling, and backpropagate through it.

  • No likelihood (§3). Diffusion gives no sample likelihood, so PPO-style RLHF fails. Classifier guidance needs a noisy-latent reward.

  • Annotation (§2.1). 8,878 DiffusionDB prompts have 4–9 images, rated on alignment, fidelity and overall, then ranked: 136,892 pairs.

  • Reward model (§2.2, Eq. 1). BLIP's ViT-L image encoder feeds its text encoder through cross-attention. An MLP maps the [CLS] feature to a scalar fθ(T,x). Every ranked pair of a prompt trains it by −𝔼logσ(fθ(T,xi)−fθ(T,xj)), with xi preferred over xj for the prompt T.

  • Frozen layers (§4.1). The model overfits fast, so the first 70% of the layers in both encoders stay frozen. The learning rate is 1e-5.

  • Late steps are identifiable (§3, Fig. 4, 19). After 30 of 40 steps, x0′ ranks seeds as the final images do: Spearman ρ 0.80 at 77.5%.

  • ReFL step (Alg. 1). Denoise without gradient to a random late step t∈[30,39] of 40. Take one step with gradient and predict x0′=(xt−1−α¯tϵθ)/α¯t, where ϵθ is the UNet's noise prediction and α¯t is the cumulative signal rate. The last step alone is unstable.

  • ReFL loss (Eq. 2). ℒreward=λReLU(2−r(y,x0′)), λ=10−3, where r is the reward normalized to zero mean and unit variance. So the push stops two standard deviations above the mean. The paper adds a pre-training loss Eq. (3) on LAION-5B, but the released code does not.

  • Experiments (§4). The pairwise accuracy is 65.14 against 57.76 for BLIP. ReFL wins 58.79% of human votes against SD v1.4.

  • FID disagrees with humans (Tab. 1). On six models, its Spearman ρ is 0.09, against 0.60 for CLIP and 1.00 for ImageReward.

def train_reward_model(blip, mlp, prompt_ids, better, worse):

    r_better = score(blip, mlp, prompt_ids, better)
    r_worse = score(blip, mlp, prompt_ids, worse)

    # 2. Calculate the pairwise ranking loss.
